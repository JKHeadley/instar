The same products on desktop

You were right that phone screenshots answer a different question. Captured on a 1440×900 desktop browser today, plus one that had to be established from documentation because every automated route is blocked. The desktop answer turns out to be the more useful one.

Grok — desktop web

grok.com, signed out

The extra space is used for nothing. One question, one input, a thin top bar. No sidebar, no panels, no dashboard. Desktop here is not a denser phone — it is an emptier one.

Meta AI — desktop web

meta.ai, signed out

Same again: a two-icon rail, a centred headline, a centred composer, and a very large amount of nothing. The rail holds new-chat and history — not categories.

Cursor Desktop — the agent console

cursor.com product shot. Cursor is where Grok Bot's admin plane now lives post-acquisition. This is the closest thing to what we are actually building.

Three panes, no tabs. Left is a task list under a heading that says what it is for — "Ready for review 5" — each row a title, a time, and one line of status with its diff. Middle is the agent's thread: a compact trace (Thought 4s · Read AppManager.tsx · Searched expose patterns), then a decision asked inline as numbered options. Right is the artifact it produced.

So the desktop space does not go to more metrics. It goes to showing you the work.

ChatGPT / Dots — desktop, documented but not photographed

No image: chatgpt.com, openai.com (three pages), help.openai.com and a third-party write-up all refuse automated browsers; the Dots announcement also returns 403 to an authenticated fetch. Seven routes, seven refusals. So this section is text evidence, clearly marked as such.

What the documentation does establish. A dot is not a tab or a pane — it sits in the sidebar, directly under "New chat", and opening one gives you a dedicated chat. The sidebar also carries conversation history and Projects, with a Chat/Work switch.

And one detail worth more than a screenshot: of the dot's cloud computer, the documentation says "you can open the computer, watch it work, or take over." That is three rungs — a status line, a preview, and a takeover — rather than a single always-on view.

So ChatGPT reaches the same place by a different road: a sidebar list of named things, each opening into a thread. No categories.

The phone screenshots said "fewer things per row". The desktop ones say something stronger: stop building a place to navigate and build a place to watch work happen. Left list of what is running and what wants you, middle the conversation, right what it produced.

That also dissolves the Work-and-Capacity problem rather than renaming it. There is no category to name, because there are no categories — just your conversations, your running work, and the thing in front of you.
