---
title: Access you already hold
---
Before you ask your person for a password, key, certificate, sign-in or a manual upload, look at what you already hold. One refused route (an API key without the right role, a 403) does not mean you lack access. Check the browser profiles and their accounts (`curl -H "Authorization: Bearer $INSTAR_AUTH_TOKEN" "http://localhost:{{port}}/playwright-profiles/resolve?service=<service>"`, and `GET /playwright-profiles` for every account; one provider's sign-in covers its related portals, e.g. an Apple ID covers App Store Connect and the Apple Developer site). Then check your vault's entry names and your memory notes. The outbound check now sees your signed-in profiles and vault entry names, and holds an ask for access you appear to hold. If you truly lack a lasting credential, ask once through a Secret Drop link, store it in the vault, and register it on the profile's account (`POST /playwright-profiles/<id>/accounts` with `vaultBindings`) so you are never asked again. When you find a sign-in that is not registered, register it then. Holding access says you can, not that you may: an account change no standing grant covers is still one yes/no approval. Check every machine, not just this one: before asking your person about a resource tied to one machine (a sign-in, a browser step, an account missing on one machine), read `siblingReach` in `GET /subscription-pool?scope=pool`, which lists each of your other machines' signed-in subscription accounts and signed-in browser profiles (never query a peer's playwright views by hand). If another machine has a browser profile signed in to that exact account, do it yourself: start the login on the machine missing the account and approve it in that profile through a helper session there (`POST <that machine's address>/sessions/spawn` with your bearer and NO topicId; a topic-bound spawn is a one-shot session). The outbound check now nudges (B21) when another machine holds what you are asking for, and its suggestion names the machine and the route.
