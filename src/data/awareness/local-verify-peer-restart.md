---
title: Local Verify helper machine works through a restart
---
If you test your own changes with Local Verify on two machines (a coordinator with a peer verifier), the peer can keep working while the coordinator waits to restart and through the restart itself. It is off unless you turn it on: set `monitoring.localVerify.peerWorksThroughRestart.enabled` to `true` in `.instar/config.json` on the coordinator (and on the peer, for its side of the progress check; read live, no restart). With it on, a pending update or planned restart no longer stops the coordinator handing a waiting PR to an idle peer, unless the verifier on disk already changed or the restart is forced; a `main` verify is never handed out then. An attempt sent during that pause does not hold that restart. A peer attempt that spans the coordinator's own restart is kept and imported when no other machine held the lease in between (audit `peer-attempt-adopted`); a different holder in between, a moved PR, a changed verifier or a `main` verify abandons it (`lease-changed:*` reasons; a changed verifier gives the attempt back once). At 60 minutes a peer attempt is judged on progress, not killed: it keeps running while files keep finishing within about twice their normal time, and ends when it stalls, when its progress cannot be read, or at 150 minutes whatever it reports (audit `peer-attempt-judged`). `curl -H "Authorization: Bearer $AUTH" http://localhost:{{port}}/local-verify` shows `peers.worksThroughRestart`, `peers.restartEpisode` and, per reserved attempt, `sentDuringPause` and `adoptedEpoch`. Setting the key to `false` is the off switch: every lease change then abandons the attempt as before, including attempts already kept. When the user asks "why did the other machine sit idle during the restart?" or "why was that peer run thrown away?" → read the audit rows `restart-episode-ended`, `peer-attempt-abandoned` and `peer-attempt-judged`.
