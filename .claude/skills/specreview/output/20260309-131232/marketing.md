## Research Findings

- **"Coherence Gate" name check**: No competitor product uses "Coherence Gate." Google search returns academic papers on quantum coherence and some network security terms, but no AI/agent product claims this name. The term is available.
- **Competitor naming landscape**: "Guardrails AI" (generic safety), "NeMo Guardrails" (NVIDIA brand + guardrails), "Lakera Guard" (guard = protection). All use safety/protection metaphors. "Coherence" occupies a different semantic space — identity and consistency rather than safety and protection.
- **AI agent quality market messaging**: Competitors message around "safety," "compliance," "risk mitigation." Nobody messages around "identity," "coherence," or "does it sound like your agent?" This is a genuine white space.
- **"Guardrails" market saturation**: The word "guardrails" appears in at least 15 AI products. It's becoming generic. "Coherence Gate" stands apart by not using the saturated term.

## Name Analysis

### Current Name: "Coherence Gate"

**Assessment**: STRONG. This name works on multiple levels:

1. **"Coherence"** captures the unique value proposition — this isn't about safety (what the agent shouldn't say), it's about identity (does it sound like this specific agent?).
2. **"Gate"** implies enforcement — this isn't a suggestion engine or a review tool, it's a gate that blocks incoherent responses.
3. **Differentiation**: Zero collision with any existing AI product name.
4. **Scalability**: The name works whether it's a feature within instar or a standalone product.
5. **Searchability**: "coherence gate" returns no competing AI products. Easy to own in SEO.

**Round 1 alternatives revisited** (for completeness):

| Alternative | Verdict |
|-------------|---------|
| Voice Gate | Still viable but narrower — implies only tone/voice, misses the claims/accuracy/values dimensions |
| Identity Filter | "Filter" implies removal, not quality enforcement. Weaker than "gate." |
| Agent Mirror | Interesting metaphor but doesn't imply enforcement |
| Signal Guard | Too similar to existing "guard" products. Doesn't capture identity angle. |

**Recommendation**: Keep "Coherence Gate." It's the right name.

## Recommendations (should fix, not blocking)

### 1. Develop the "Three Types of AI Quality" Framework (HIGH)
Position the Coherence Gate within a clear framework that makes the market legible:

| Type | What It Checks | Who Does It | Example |
|------|---------------|-------------|---------|
| **Safety** | "Will this harm someone?" | Guardrails AI, Lakera, NeMo | Block harmful/toxic content |
| **Accuracy** | "Is this factually correct?" | Promptfoo, Braintrust, eval tools | Measure against ground truth |
| **Coherence** | "Does this sound like the agent?" | **Coherence Gate** (instar) | Enforce identity, values, channel-awareness |

This framework positions Coherence Gate as complementary to (not competing with) existing tools while establishing a new category. The tagline already does this: "Guardrails stop dangerous things. Coherence Gate stops things that don't sound like it."

### 2. The Organic Evolution Narrative (HIGH)
**Section**: Organic Evolution

The self-healing coherence loop is the strongest narrative element added in Round 2. It transforms the product from "a quality filter" to "a system that learns from every mistake, gets smarter over time, and shares learnings across all agents." This is the story that resonates with technical audiences:

- "Every user complaint makes your agent smarter"
- "Your agent's coherence improves automatically — not just when you tune prompts"
- "What one agent learns, every agent benefits from"

This is the network effect story. It should be prominent in marketing materials.

### 3. Publish the Dawn Incidents (HIGH, CONTENT MARKETING)
The incident appendix is the single best content marketing asset. It validates the problem with real stories, not hypotheticals. Specific pitch: "9 Ways AI Agents Embarrass Themselves (And How to Stop It)":

1. The Sleep Theory — when your agent invents causes for failures
2. The Config Dump — when your agent talks like a developer
3. The Localhost Link — when your agent forgets who's reading
4. The File-and-Wait — when your agent forgets who it is
5. The Fabricated URL — when your agent lies, then lies about lying
6. The Stale Draft — when your agent publishes yesterday's thinking
7. The Email Nuke — when "clean up" means "delete everything"
8. The Telephone Game — when errors amplify through handoffs
9. The Notification Storm — when infrastructure speaks for the agent

Each incident demonstrates a real failure, links to the Coherence Gate reviewer that catches it, and builds demand for the product.

### 4. Recipient-Aware Messaging (MEDIUM)
**Section**: Recipient-Aware Grounding

Four recipient types with different review strictness is a unique selling point. The marketing angle: "Your agent knows that an email to a client needs different quality standards than a Slack message to your team." This resonates with enterprise buyers who worry about agents sending inappropriate content to external contacts.

### 5. The Migration Story (MEDIUM)
**Section**: Migration and Rollout Plan

The 5-week phased migration with observe-only start is a strong adoption message: "Zero-risk adoption. Watch for 2 weeks. Enable gradually. Roll back anytime." This directly addresses the #1 objection to any AI quality tool: "What if it blocks legitimate content?"

## Observations

1. **The tagline is already perfect.** "Guardrails stop your agent from saying dangerous things. The Coherence Gate stops it from saying things that don't sound like it." This is a one-sentence category definition. Don't change it.

2. **The cost story is a non-objection.** $3-6/month per agent. "Less than a cup of coffee per month for agent quality assurance." This removes the primary adoption barrier — nobody's going to cost-optimize against $3/month.

3. **The "Coherence" term is defensible against incumbents.** If Guardrails AI wanted to add identity-grounded review, they'd have to explain why "guardrails" includes "coherence" — a stretch. The category name is the moat.

4. **The organic evolution system is a potential standalone narrative.** "Self-healing AI quality" could be a conference talk, a blog post series, or a product positioning pillar. It's novel enough to generate press interest.

5. **The custom reviewer marketplace potential** (from Business review) has marketing implications. "Build your own quality rules" and "Share industry-specific reviewers" are community-building hooks.
