# Review round 3 (visual) — GPT-6 Astra (codex CLI), 2026-10-25

**Verdict: close.** The console unmistakably belongs to Instar: the navy ground, faint grid, crab mark, Inter headings, teal accents and restrained borders match the supplied website reference. Desktop has a clear primary column, and the Work directory has coherent icons and generous tile targets. It is not yet at the operator's “extremely aesthetic” production bar on a phone: important qualifications are faint, small actions are fiddly, and repeated card spacing makes Home feel longer than its information warrants.

This review uses all four supplied screenshots, the current console source, the plan and both reviewers' rounds 1–2. The fixed navigation appearing mid-page in the full-page captures is not a defect. Different live counts between captures are not a defect either. Failure-state observations below are identified as source findings; those states are not pictured. No application code was changed.

Ranked defects and smallest fixes:

1. **High — Uncertainty loses the visual argument to confident headlines.** The promises card admits “some of it is out of date” only below its rows, while the hero presents an unqualified current total. More seriously, a direct evaluation of `home-model.js` confirms that six unknown cards produce “Mostly quiet” with an `ok` tone; a guard-only attention state can produce “All quiet.” These are presentation rules, independent of whether the live records are correct. **Smallest fix:** in `headline()` and `paintHeadline()`, reserve healthy wording and teal treatment for fully observed, settled state; count attention outside the two queue cards and retain a coverage qualification alongside any known total. In `buildPromised()` / `paintCard()`, surface stale or unverified coverage beside the value as a visible status label, while retaining the amber action count. A card can need action and have incomplete coverage simultaneously.

2. **High — Useful secondary text is too faint at phone size.** Machine names beneath rows, timestamps, the stale-promises qualification and inactive navigation labels approach background texture. Copying the website's faintest token does not make it suitable for operational evidence at 11–12.5 px. **Smallest fix:** in `console.css`, use `--text-muted` for `.row-sub`, `.row-end`, `.card-age`, `.card-note`, `.machine .m-load` and phone `.nav a`; set essential row metadata to at least 13 px and navigation labels to 12 px. Keep the quieter token for decorative eyebrows. Preserve the existing primary text sizes.

3. **High — Truncated promises offer no clear way to read the missing text.** The phone shows several two-line fragments precisely where the operator needs to understand the request. “See all 50” reveals more rows, but the same clamp remains. In source, promise rows have no `detail`, and attention rows become expandable only when a separate description/body exists; the full title alone does not qualify. Expandable rows also lack a visible disclosure cue. **Smallest fix:** in `home-model.js`, provide the full text as `detail` for these rows even when there is no separate body. In `console.js` `paintCard()`, add a small chevron and `aria-expanded`/`aria-controls` to the row button. Keep `.row-title` clamped only in its collapsed state, and preserve expanded row IDs across the 30-second repaint. This uses the existing disclosure mechanism.

4. **Medium — Card actions and the Legacy chip are undersized touch targets.** “Open” and “See all” look delicate but are essentially text-height controls; `.link-btn` has zero padding, and `.chip` is only 30 px high. The bottom navigation and Work tiles already provide much better targets. **Smallest fix:** in the phone rules in `console.css`, give `.card-foot a` and `.link-btn` an inline-flex hit area with `min-height: 44px`, `min-width: 44px` and modest horizontal padding; give `.topbar .chip` a 44 px target. Use existing footer whitespace and reduce compensating gaps so this does not lengthen every card unnecessarily.

5. **Medium — Uncontrolled heading margins inflate the repeated card rhythm.** Card labels sit in a conspicuously tall band above their values. The `.card-title` is an `h2` with no margin reset, so browser heading margins add space on top of `.card`'s 12 px gap. Repeated six times, this contributes substantially to the phone scroll. Work's two-line heading likewise inherits the body line-height of 1.55, making its masthead unusually loose compared with the site. **Smallest fix:** set `.card-title { margin: 0; line-height: 1.3; }` and `.group-head h1 { line-height: 1.15; }` in `console.css`. Keep the existing card padding and reassess before shrinking any body text or tiles.

6. **Medium — Three broad amber surfaces compete with the intended focal card.** On desktop, needs, promises and permissions receive almost identical tinted panels and outlines. On the phone this repeats a yellow-heavy treatment through Home, weakening both the first card's priority and the site's restrained teal identity. Amber is appropriate for these real attention states; its painted area is the issue. **Smallest fix:** in `console.css`, retain the current wash on `.card.feature[data-state="attn"]`, but use a neutral surface and a weaker amber border on other attention cards. Keep their amber numbers and status dots. This establishes priority without changing the information order or removing warning colour.

7. **Medium — Machine evidence changes meaning within one column.** The rightmost value is `1.1/core` for Mac Studio but “just now” for peers. Load replaces age rather than accompanying it, so the machine with the most telemetry loses its freshness evidence. **Smallest fix:** in `console.js` `paintCard()`'s machine markup, always render `mc.age`; when load exists, put it in a separate small line beneath that age within the existing final cell. Style that cell in `console.css` as a right-aligned two-line stack. No extra phone column is needed.

Earlier-round plan issues still open:

- **The honesty contract is only partly fulfilled (Astra r1 #3, r2 #2; Argon r2 #4).** The mesh query now correctly fetches all statuses, but seeing replicas from three origins does not establish complete coverage, and missing replica ages are not treated as unknown. Guard/mandate handling also still substitutes zero for missing fields: directly evaluating `buildAllowed()` with successful `{summary:{}}` and `{}` responses produces an `ok` “0 of 0 on” card and “0 permission slips active.” A failed mandate read gets explanatory row text but does not necessarily make the card partial; a failed guard read discards a successful mandate result. Require explicit observed fields, keep whichever source answered, and retain coverage uncertainty independently of action severity. The reach card's large “Reaching you” also still exceeds its narrower evidence, “No replies are held on this machine”; change the large label to “No replies held.” These remain small view-model/copy fixes, not reasons to change architecture.

- **The plan has not been reconciled with fixes already in the code.** `docs/specs/console-makeover.md` still specifies the filtered commitments query, a universal five-second deadline, “last tool use,” overlapping machine predicates, collapsed Under the hood links, and obsolete detail destinations. Code now has the unfiltered query, eight-second pool deadline, explicit machine precedence, visible Settings links and inline attention/guard lists. Update the plan to those actual contracts and describe session telemetry as recorded activity; the focus endpoint is not currently fetched. The promised light-theme toggle is absent from `renderShell()` despite retained theme code; record the earlier reviewers' suggested dark-only MVP deferral instead of claiming a toggle exists.

- **Detail completeness and interaction acceptance remain unfinished (Astra r1 #3/#5, r2 #1/#6).** Inline lists largely resolve the wrong-destination finding, but `paintCard()` silently caps an expanded “See all” list at 60. Remove the cap or expose a truthful “Show next” control. The current lifecycle test exercises HTTP and view-models rather than browser interactions. These screenshots establish appearance, not keyboard/PIN usability, refresh preservation, 360 px behaviour or the appearance of partial/stale/failure states. Keep those checks in phone acceptance, including a reading interrupted by refresh. Cutover query/hash preservation remains a later switch gate, as planned.

The earlier iframe, framing-header, legacy-reskin and operator-proof handoff concerns are resolved by ordinary legacy links. No rewrite, framework change or additional design round is needed to address the findings above; Justin's phone acceptance remains the final visual decision.
