## ELI10 Overview

Today Justin is the only thing that keeps an agent's projects moving. He starts a project in its own conversation, then has to come back to nudge it, check it hasn't drifted, and restart a "24-hour session" so it keeps going. With dozens of projects that follow-up job has itself become unmanageable, and the agent's own project list proves it: 24 active projects, 20 of them untouched for weeks or months.

The spec gives every agent one special conversation, the **Chief of Staff**. It sits pinned at the top of the app and dashboard, every other conversation hangs under it in a tree, and it is the operator's go-to place. On a schedule it checks each project against the goal and definition of done the operator confirmed, decides whether it is moving, stalled, drifting, waiting on the operator, or finished, and acts: restart stalled work, point drifting work back at its goal, put questions into one short daily note, verify "done" before closing. It keeps acting only while the operator has checked in with it recently; otherwise it pauses.

The main trade-off is authority versus brakes. Justin granted the Chief of Staff the right to restart and redirect on its own, so the review spent most of its effort on making sure it can never restart work something else already owns, never burn quota restarting a dead project forever, never be kept alive by anyone but the owner, and never certify "done" on a project's say-so.

## Original vs Converged

- **Storage.** The first draft put goals and check state on the existing project records. Review showed those records live in a local database that is explicitly not synced between machines, so a machine hand-off would lose them. The converged design uses a new replicated store, registered exactly the way the existing replicated kinds are, with never-delete retention because the substrate's rotation would otherwise throw away the records the brakes depend on.
- **One owner per run.** The draft said the Chief of Staff "consumes" the existing auto-restart machinery. Review showed the two would race. The converged design has an explicit stand-down rule (anything that already owns the run, including an unexpired claim from a previous lease epoch, blocks the Chief of Staff), a claim record written before every action, a lock on the machine that actually executes, fencing on the lease epoch at execution, and crash recovery against the run registry. Three of those pieces did not exist in source and the spec now names them as build scope rather than assuming them.
- **What counts as progress.** The draft counted any commit or PR state change. Review showed that let a session fake progress with churn. Now only landed changes count: a merge, or a linked branch's net diff passing its previous high-water mark; chat and churn are "activity" and never reset the brakes.
- **The brakes.** The draft said restarts were "capped" without saying how. Now: three restarts without progress parks a project; a breaker trips after a configurable number per week regardless of progress and only the owner can clear it; backoff doubles the cadence; at most two restarts per tick agent-wide; everything through the existing quota and pressure gates.
- **The safety switch.** The draft let "a tap on a note" renew the check-in lease. Now only the owner's authenticated principal renews it, the owner check happens once on the machine that received the event, the record is machine-signed, and expiry is derived on read, never stored.
- **Done means done.** The draft let an LLM read "I'm done". Now deterministic checklist items are verified by the Chief of Staff, judgment items go to the owner unless the owner delegated that item at binding time, and no verdict with authority rests on conversation text alone.
- **Noise.** The draft sent a daily note always. Now a note goes out only when there is a result, something that needs the owner, or something the Chief of Staff cannot see; routine counts live on the status page.
- **Nothing parked on the user.** Three open questions (name, lease length, note time) became config defaults the operator can change.

## Convergence verdict

Converged at round 6, the Tier-2 cap, with the honest caveat stated in Proportion: the final round still produced DESIGN findings, every one of them in the §2.4 execution/coordination protocol, and three of them were safety or data-loss class. Per the cap rule those could not be accepted as risk, so each was fixed with a direct text change from its finding and the result was checked by a verification read rather than a seventh review round. No design finding outside §2.4 survived round 4. Precision items are listed under Left behind. The spec is ready for operator review and approval, and the build should begin with the §2.4 protocol and its concurrency/idempotency tests (test-agent-live categories: concurrency, idempotency, failure/rollback, lifecycle) before any user-visible surface.
