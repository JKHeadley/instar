# Agent work cards by default: plain-English overview

## What this change is

Instar agents keep a "Work board": a list of cards, each one a piece of work with a checklist, an owner, and its own chat thread. "Needs you" is the list in the app and on the console of things waiting for a person. When you ask your agent for something, the agent should have a card for that work: what you asked, the steps to finish it, the agent's own notes as it goes, and a close-out when it's done. You should be able to look at those cards under the project they belong to, without being asked to approve or decide anything. That should be how every Instar agent behaves, out of the box.

This came from Justin on 20 November 2026: "I would hope that there is a built-in mechanism on which you take all of that, process it and turn it into action items or cards that you are responsible for ... so that nothing is lost or forgotten ... I would expect this to be a default behavior for all Instar agents." Later the same evening he added two things he uses daily on another agent's board: cards should have owners who can be any team member and can hand a card to someone else, and a card should be able to be blocked by another card. And: a request he makes directly should become a card, never a "feedback" report sent off to the Instar maintainers.

## What already exists

The Work board already has almost all the pieces: a lane for cards the agent is working on, checklists where some items the agent can tick itself and some only you can, a conversation on every card, cards listed under each project in the app, an owner field on every card, and a board setting called "Only outward cards wait" under which the agent starts internal work at once and only asks you about things that go outside your team or cost money. The default setting is the strict one: every card the agent writes waits for your OK. Switching to the lighter setting is a trust decision, so it can only happen through the existing approval request (a message in your chat you answer with a code, or a tap on your paired phone).

## What's wrong today

None of it is the default, and the agent has to remember to use it:

- Every card the agent writes is treated as a request for your OK, even for work you asked for yourself. When the agent opened two tracking cards for Justin's feedback today, both showed up in his Needs you list, which is exactly the noise he had just asked to remove.
- Nothing automatically turns a request into a card. Whether one exists depends on the agent thinking of it, and one of Justin's direct requests was filed as "feedback" to the maintainers instead.
- The agent cannot write notes on its own card from the place it usually works (a Telegram conversation), and cannot create a card for you from there either; the only write path is the phone's.
- A task that doesn't belong to any project has nowhere to live.
- Only the team owner can own a card, and nothing can mark a card as blocked by another.
- Every team starts, and stays, on the strict setting, and nothing ever offers the lighter one.

## What's new

1. **Your own request is the yes.** When you, the team owner, ask for something in your own chat, from your own Telegram account or from your own paired phone through the app, that message is the approval for the agent to do that internal work. The card starts at once, under either setting, and you are never asked to approve it, however long ago you asked. If the agent records one message of yours as two pieces of work, both start. What you asked is pinned on the card as "Asked: ..." in full and cannot be edited; the card shows how many days ago you asked, and says "recorded in the agent's words" if what the agent wrote down was not your exact words, or "changed since asked" if it later changed the title or checklist, so you can see any drift. Only turning it into outward work (sending or spending) or handing it to a person brings the normal approval back. The agent checks who really sent the message in its stored record, not just which chat it came from: for a phone message it checks the phone's own registration on the computer that holds it. A forwarded message, a message from a team member in your group, a message from a member's phone, or a message the record cannot vouch for is treated like a member's request (point 2).
2. **Other agent-work cards ask nothing either.** A card for work a team member asked for, or that the agent started itself, never appears as its own line in Needs you, Telegram, Slack or as a push. Under "Only outward cards wait" the agent starts it at once. Under the strict setting it sits under the project marked "not started", the agent does no real work on it (it tells the requester the owner has to approve), and your Needs you shows **one** line for the whole team while any such cards exist: "N internal cards are waiting for your approval or board setting", which expands to show each card in full (what, who asked, the checklist, the links) and lets you approve the ones shown, or approve them and widen what the agent may start on its own. Only you can use those buttons. If you answer neither, those cards stay visible, counted in that one line, and unworked; your own requests are never affected.
3. **Every direct request becomes a card automatically.** The moment the agent records a promise to you from one of your conversations, Instar writes the card itself, under the right project, with the milestones the agent gave or a single "Done: ..." item it refines later. One promise, one card: the card's identity comes from the promise record, so a retry or a second computer cannot make a second one. If two cards might be the same request, the agent is told and decides. If the first write fails for any reason that can be retried, a background sweep finishes it within minutes, using your own words even if the computer that took the request is off; the same sweep catches up recent open promises once when the feature arrives (older ones stay in the usual promise review). A direct request can no longer be sent to the maintainers' feedback queue by habit: feedback now has to say where it came from, and "from the operator" is refused with a pointer to the card route. If the agent answers you in chat and takes on work without recording it, the check that already reads every outgoing reply marks that; a few minutes later a plain lookup checks whether a promise was recorded for the exact message the agent was answering, and only if none was does it file one into the existing promise review (a guess that never sends you a reminder) and remind the agent in its next turn and its next briefing in that conversation. Never a block, and never on the quick "on it" acknowledgement.
4. **No second worker.** The conversation that took your request is already doing the work, so a card made this way is held: nothing is dispatched to it elsewhere while that conversation is still running (whether it is a Telegram, Slack or app conversation); if something does need the agent (an answer you gave, a card that unblocked), it is delivered into that same conversation, and if that conversation has ended, one ordinary pickup happens instead. A card with no activity for a day gets one automatic pickup first, delivered into the running conversation if there still is one; only if that happens twice does it show up for you (a busy system waiting its turn does not count). Pickups happen one at a time, so an approval of many cards, a settings change or an update never starts a wave of sessions.
5. **Milestones, notes, close-out.** Milestones are the card's checklist. The agent writes progress notes on the card from the conversation doing the work or any of its sessions, and they appear in the card's conversation immediately, on every computer; if the system is too busy at that instant the note is refused and the agent writes it again a moment later, so a note is never written to one computer only. Notes are capped in size (you set the cap); above fifty a day on one card the card's own log just points at the conversation in batches, so nothing is ever dropped. When the agent has shown evidence for every item it can check itself, the card closes; the close-out says "with a link or attachment" when the evidence includes one and "on its own word" when it does not, so you can tell what it rests on. A step only you can judge waits for your tap, as today. Sending a reply is not proof the work is done, and the agent has to say which item its evidence is for.
6. **A home for tasks with no project.** They go into a "General" section. When the agent judges that several belong together, it suggests a project once; only you can accept that. No word-matching or magic number decides it.
7. **The lighter setting, offered through you.** The default stays strict for work you did not ask for yourself. If your team already exists, your agent asks you exactly once, in your own chat or on your phone, whether to switch, and the ask shows what it rests on (how many other internal cards you have approved, changed or declined so far), says plainly that it widens what the agent starts on its own, and says it covers future internal cards. For any team, the "Approve and widen" button on the Needs you line (or a card) sends that same request with the same wording; on your own paired phone you see it and one tap approves it, otherwise it tells you to answer in chat or on the phone. Only your yes through that request switches it. The request queue also stops counting requests that were never delivered against its limit, and the agent can withdraw a request nobody has answered (and cannot re-ask the same thing by withdrawing and re-sending).
8. **Card owners.** Any team member can own a card, not just the team owner. The owner of a card, or the team owner, can pass it to another member or to the agent; the agent can pass a card it holds to a member only as a proper ask (with a recommendation and why it is for them), and can never take a card from a person. The new owner sees it in their own Needs you and in the card's chat; nobody else is notified. The person who sent a request can see its card; otherwise a card is visible only to people who could already see its conversation, and only you (the team owner) can share it wider. A member handing a card to the agent does not skip your approval under the strict setting. If someone leaves the team, each card they owned comes back to your Needs you with a "Pass" button, passing it clears that, they stop seeing cards they owned or asked for, and rejoining does not hand the cards back by itself.
9. **Blocked cards.** A card can be marked as blocked by another card. When the agent's work needs something from you, the very conversation doing the work writes a card for you saying what it needs, linked in the same step as blocking its own card, so a hiccup cannot leave a card without its link. A card can wait on at most a handful of such asks at once (you set the number), and every ask counts against the agent's daily card allowance, so a stuck loop cannot flood anyone's list. Your card shows "Blocks: ..." and the agent's shows "Blocked by: ...". When you close yours, the agent's card wakes up and the agent is briefed (including whether you closed it as done or "not important", and any note). Loops ("A blocks B blocks A") are refused. A blocked card never hides a decision that is waiting for you. Someone who can see only one of the two cards is never shown the other card's title or notes, and cannot probe whether a card exists. Reopening a card you closed does not re-block anything; the agent is told and decides.
10. **In the app and the console.** Under each project: your asks first, then the agent's work cards folded away as a secondary list, each showing its owner, whether it is blocked or not started, what was asked and when, and how it was closed. The project row only shows a "needs you" line when there's a real ask for you, and it names it. These screens are built and driven on the simulator as part of the work, not left for you to discover.

## Safeguards, in plain terms

- The agent still can't approve, confirm or archive anything; your own verified request approves the work you asked for, your tap approves the rest, and the setting switch only happens through the approval request you answer.
- The "outward" rule still holds: anything that sends outside your team or spends money waits for you, and the separate safety gate on external actions doesn't change at all.
- Closing a card needs evidence on every item, never just the agent saying "done", and the close-out label says what the evidence was.
- Cards, notes and links are never deleted or dropped; a closed card and its notes stay readable.
- Nothing the agent or the server does can show a card to someone who couldn't already see it; only you can share.
- If your agent runs on several computers, the new kinds of card records are written only once every computer that is actually around (or was seen in the last three days, which you can change) reports the update as its current version; a computer that is gone or long silent does not hold things up, one that was rolled back lowers the bar again, and one that comes back on an old version stops reading the shared card records until it updates.

## What you need to decide

Nothing before this is built. Your own requests will simply start as cards. The one decision that may reach you afterwards is whether to switch your team to "Only outward cards wait" for the other internal cards (the one-time message for an existing team, or the "Approve and widen" button); saying nothing keeps the strict setting for those, and the one Needs you line tells you how many are waiting on it.
