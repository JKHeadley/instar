# Meeting-transcript analysis as a built-in skill

## Summary

Today an agent that sits in on a client call writes its "post-meeting read" (what was decided, who does what, what the client still owes) from one pass over the transcript, or worse, from the recording service's summary. That misses things that matter: on 2026-11-19 the agent Inspec turned "build Paul a mock site with the real chat widget" into "send Paul screenshots" because it worked from two meeting summaries instead of the transcript and kept the vaguer one when they disagreed. This spec makes Luna's measured method a built-in skill for every agent on every framework: read the full transcript, file every line with its exact words, flag every place two sources disagree, quote the transcript for every extracted item, and have fresh tool-less readers check the result until a round finds nothing. Small scripts refuse to pass when anything is unaccounted for. Client material stays private to the meeting's owner and in the place the owner chose. It ships dark, starts on development agents and Luna, and reaches the fleet only after real meetings the operator reviews with nothing missing.

## Glossary

- **Luna:** the SageMind agent (`@luna_sagemind_bot`). She wrote and runs this method for client calls, in her skill `.claude/skills/gci-post-call`.
- **The package:** `sagemind/project_sandbox/instar-transcript-skill` on the Studio, sent by Luna on Threadline thread `1f805ef5` on 2026-11-06 and updated after runs on 2026-11-09 and 2026-11-13. It holds the general skill text, the interface, five check scripts, a window splitter, a ledger builder, a shared library and 27 tests (`node --test test/*.test.mjs`: 27/27 pass, run 2026-11-17 PT).
- **Operator:** the human who owns the client relationship and is told the result.
- **Recorder:** whatever captured the call. It produces the transcript and sometimes screenshots, highlights and notes ("marks").
- **Meeting folder:** one folder per meeting run, inside the client workspace, holding every input and output of the run.
- **Ledger:** one row per stretch of transcript lines, with classes, a verbatim quote and the item it supports.
- **Blind readers:** two fresh readers that see only the transcript, each with a different method; their lists are compared with the ledger.
- **Verifier rounds:** fresh readers given the transcript, the marks read and the finished read, asked what is missing or wrong.
- **Hook:** a per-agent script named in the agent's client config that does a client-specific step (fetch the recording, stage changes, tell the operator).
- **Item-changing finding:** a verifier finding that adds, removes or changes an action, owner, ask, decision, priority or referent. **Firmness finding:** one about how firm a yes was, who a remark was addressed to, or a qualifier, that changes none of those.
- **EXO 3.0 Agent Specification:** the eight-property agent spec from *The Organizational Singularity* (Salim Ismail, OS Outline v24/v25). Instar already ships two EXO skills (`agent-readiness`, `agent-passport`).

## Problem statement

Agents that sit in on client calls are asked for a "post-meeting read": what was decided, who does what, what the client still owes. One pass over a long transcript misses things, and the misses are costly: a direct ask to the agent buried in small talk, a "this" whose referent decides the priority, a dropped qualifier that turns "spend little" into "spend none". Luna measured it. On her first full run, seven fresh verifier rounds over a read that already looked complete found 46 more points, 35 of them item-changing or priority wording (ROUNDS.md). On 2026-11-09 the read had about 30 items the recording service's own notes dropped, and the service's notes had 4 errors. On 2026-11-13 the operator's in-call screenshots carried facts the words did not.

Luna built a layered method with scripts that refuse to pass when a line, a keyword hit, a blind-reader item, a mapping or a recorder mark is unaccounted for. Today it exists only in her agent home and the package. Luna relayed (thread 1f805ef5, 2026-11-06) that Justin wants it built into Instar for every agent. Echo has not had that from Justin directly, so this spec goes to him for approval before any build (CMT-1406).

**What the checks prove, and what they don't.** The scripts prove structural facts: every line sits in a row, every keyword hit and blind-reader item is covered on its own line or carries a written verdict, every item maps to something that exists, every quote is verbatim, every mark has a read entry, the run record is consistent with its files. They do not prove the read is right. Correctness comes from the readers and verifiers, and is measured on fixtures with planted misses (Tests) and by misses the operator finds afterwards.

## Proposed design

### 1. One procedure, every framework

- The procedure ships as one built-in skill, `meeting-transcript-analysis`, content in `MEETING_TRANSCRIPT_ANALYSIS_SKILL_CONTENT` (`src/data/builtinSkillContent.ts`). The interface (inputs, outputs, hooks) and the EXO source passage are appendices inside the same SKILL.md, so the single file is the complete procedure; nothing else needs an installer.
- `installBuiltinSkills()` writes `.claude/skills/meeting-transcript-analysis/SKILL.md` (install-if-missing). `migrateBuiltinSkills` writes the canonical `.instar/skills/meeting-transcript-analysis/SKILL.md` and `migrateBuiltinSkillRenderings` renders it for Codex (`.agents/skills/`). The build adds a direct canonical write and Codex rendering to the fresh Codex-only `init` path (today it skips, `init.ts` ~553/960, and `canonicalSkillsRootFor` derives the root from `.claude/skills`), and a test checks the frontmatter survives `normalizeSkillFrontmatter`. Gemini agents have no skill rendering; the awareness entry (fleet step) points them at the canonical file to read.
- Fresh readers (blind readers, verifiers) are started by the shipped helper `fresh-read.mjs` (item 3), which uses the agent's own framework CLI through an adapter table. **Every engine Instar supports has an adapter at ship: Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI and instar-native (pi).** Each adapter is built from the same argument and environment builders Instar's own intelligence provider for that engine uses (`ClaudeCliIntelligenceProvider`, `CodexCliIntelligenceProvider`, `buildGeminiOneShotArgv`/`buildGeminiChildEnv`, `PiCliIntelligenceProvider`), plus that engine's no-tools settings (Gemini: an empty scratch folder, a settings file that excludes every core tool, `--extensions none`, no MCP servers; pi: its no-tools mode). Framework-optimizing: where an engine accepts image input (Claude Code, Codex at ship) the verifier also gets the screenshots; on the others, screenshot facts follow the held-evidence rule in item 2a, so the read still completes. An engine whose adapter has not passed the no-tool-use injection test on this machine (test-agent rung, Tests) gets `no-fresh-reader` for that engine, with the reason; that is a guard for an engine added later, not a state any shipped engine is in, and adding the adapter is part of onboarding a new engine (the framework-issues playbook). No framework-specific step lives in the procedure text.
- **No hand-kept copies.** The installed scripts cannot import Instar's TypeScript, so the build generates one file, `generated-constants.mjs`, from the canonical definitions: `DURABLE_SECRET_PATTERNS` and each provider's argument/environment builder output (the providers export their builders for this). The generator runs in `pnpm build` beside the standards-registry asset generator, and CI fails when the committed output is stale. Nobody edits two places when a safety boundary changes, and there is no parity test to keep green by hand.

### 2. Changes to the method (from the reviews and from Luna's own later runs)

- **Neutral verifier question.** Verifiers are not told the read is incomplete. They are asked: "List anything missing, misattributed (speaker, owner, referent), misquoted or overstated, with line numbers and quotes. An empty list is a valid answer; say what you checked." (The package's verifiers were told the read was incomplete; no finding was ever rejected and no round came back clean.)
- **Two lists, one counts.** Verifiers return item-changing findings and firmness findings separately. Only item-changing findings oblige another round; firmness findings are applied when they hold up and shown in the report, and the agent may judge another round not worth the spend for them alone (the package's 2026-11-09 lesson 4). But a run that stops with any new firmness finding still open is reported `INCOMPLETE (firmness open)`, never converged or ready: the audit stopped before a pass found nothing new. A firmness finding that turns out to change an action's existence, owner or priority is item-changing.
- **Every source is compared, and the transcript wins.** Every non-transcript source the run has (the recorder's notes, the operator's summary, a participant's or the client's summary, prior meeting reads) is compared item by item with the read. Each disagreement (an item one source has and another lacks, a different owner, referent, ask or firmness) is listed in a "Where sources disagree" section of the report with the transcript quote that settles it. A summary is never the basis of an item: an item exists only with a transcript quote (or a verified mark), and when two summaries disagree the agent does not pick the vaguer or the firmer one, it goes to the transcript. This is the Inspec incident (2026-11-19) made into a check: `run-check --final` fails a read whose items lack a transcript or mark citation, or whose source comparison does not cover every item of every source the run fetched. The comparison is checked against the source itself, not against an extractor's list: `run-check` splits each fetched source deterministically into units (each bullet, line or sentence of its text; each entry of a structured vendor note) and requires one comparison entry per unit: matched to a read item (with its id), a disagreement (with the settling transcript quote), or not an item (greeting, logistics, small talk, duplicate of unit N, each with a short reason). A unit with no entry fails the check, so a partial extraction cannot pass. A source whose units are all honestly "not an item" (a greetings-only note) passes, and the report records it as "compared, nothing to extract". An empty comparison for a source with units fails.
- **Reversals and disputes: the agent settles what evidence can settle.** When a finding reverses an earlier round's finding about the same item and field, or two sources disagree and the transcript is ambiguous, the agent gathers the evidence and weighs all of it together, not only the first source that looks decisive: (1) the transcript quotes; (2) verified marks (screenshots read by an image-capable reader); (3) the authorized project records the `gatherContext` hook supplies (prep notes, agenda, open items, prior meeting reads, the client's own written request, and any written correction made after the call); (4) targeted fresh verifiers asked only that question with all the evidence (as many as the agent judges useful, within the run's spend ceiling). A later written correction from the person who owns the decision outranks an in-call remark only when it is explicit, and the report shows both. A settlement records the sources weighed and the quotes. Only when no rung settles it is the item and field a dispute: both readings stay in the read, every action, staged change and project-file change that depends on the disputed field is held, and the operator gets one question with both readings and their quotes (the package's 2026-11-13 lesson 6, without refusing correct fixes, and without asking the operator what the records could answer).
- **How much reading, and when the loop stops** (judgment within floors; Decision points touched). Blind readers: at least one, with a method different from the main read; two by default, and the agent may use one for a short call (it records the reason in `run.json.readerPlan`). Verifier rounds: no fixed count. The floors are evidence floors, not counts: `converged` needs a round with zero findings of either kind from a fresh reader; and a round that led to any item-changing fix is always followed by another round, because the fix itself has not been checked (the recorded runs' findings rose again after falling, 4 then 5 in ROUNDS.md, which is why a fixed read is not trusted unchecked). So a short, clear call whose first round is clean is `converged` after one round; a call that keeps changing keeps being checked. A run never ends on an unchecked edit: firmness findings that are applied are checked by one more full round over the whole read (with the edited lines pointed out), so an edit that leaves a dependent item elsewhere inconsistent is caught; firmness findings that are only listed (not applied) do not need a round. Without that last check the run's status is `INCOMPLETE`. Honest statuses: `converged` only after a round with zero new findings of either kind (Iterative Audit to Convergence); `settled-with-limits` the same, with some verification unavailable (listed); otherwise `INCOMPLETE`, with the reason: `firmness open` (the last round found firmness findings and the agent stopped), `quota` or `ceiling` (item-changing findings may be left). Every open finding is listed. The agent judges whether another round is worth it while only firmness findings remain; stopping is allowed, calling it finished is not. The operator can order more rounds.
- **2a. Status table** (one table; `run-check` computes it from the last round and the run record):

  Run status precedence: any blocking failure gives `blocked`; otherwise an `INCOMPLETE` analysis gives `incomplete`; otherwise any dispute or non-blocking hook failure gives `ready-with-disputes`; otherwise `ready`. A `ready` or `ready-with-disputes` run stays `ready-pending-replication` until another machine confirms it holds the commitments and evidence (order of work, step 6); a single-machine agent's status says it is on one machine. `incomplete` is reported as incomplete in the report and the operator notification, never as done. Verification limits are always listed in the report, whatever the status. The analysis status comes from the last round and from what verification was available: `converged` needs a zero-finding last round with every applicable check available; a zero-finding last round with some verification unavailable (no marks fetched, no image-capable reader) is `settled-with-limits`, and the limits are listed.

  **Held evidence (unknown is not clean).** A change is never staged on evidence that is still unknown. When marks were fetched but no image-capable reader checked them, every item whose lines carry a mark, and every change depending on such an item, is held like a disputed field until a reader checks the picture or the operator confirms. When `recorderExtras` is not configured, whether the call had screenshots is unknown. Then the agent judges each client-visible change. A change resting on two independent sources (an explicit transcript quote and a written record from the `gatherContext` hook, such as the client's own written request) may be staged. A change resting on the call alone may also be staged when a fresh reviewer (a targeted tool-less verifier given the transcript and the change) confirms the request on the call is unambiguous, and the change is reversible and stays staged behind the operator's publishing word. In both cases the agent records why unseen screenshots could not change it; every other change is held. Staging is never publishing, so the operator still sees it first. Absence of marks counts as known only when the `fetchTranscript` hook reports, from the recorder's own metadata for that meeting, that no marks were captured; a config value alone does not lift a hold. The report names each held change and the evidence it waits on.

  | Analysis status (last round) | Disputes | Hooks and checks | Run status | May stage? |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | `converged` (zero findings, nothing unavailable) | none | all pass | `ready` | yes |
  | `settled-with-limits` | none | all pass | `ready` (limits listed) | yes, except changes resting on unchecked evidence (held, see Held evidence) |
  | `converged` or `settled-with-limits` | some | all pass | `ready-with-disputes` | yes, except changes depending on a disputed field (held) |
  | `INCOMPLETE` (firmness open, quota or ceiling; open findings listed) | any | all pass | `incomplete`, with every open finding treated as a dispute | only changes no open finding or dispute touches |
  | `converged` or `settled-with-limits` | any | a non-blocking hook failed (`updateProjectFiles`, `recorderNotes`, `drafts`, `notifyOperator` after retry) | `ready-with-disputes`, the failure listed | yes, except what the failed hook feeds |
  | `INCOMPLETE` | any | a non-blocking hook failed | `incomplete`, the failure also listed | only changes no open finding, dispute or failed hook touches |
  | any | any | a check failed, live surface not unchanged, sync or lock wrong, `no-fresh-reader`, `fetchTranscript` or `recorderExtras` or `stageChanges` failed | `blocked` | no |

- **Quotes in the read are checked too.** The meeting read's own quotes go through the same verbatim check as the ledger's (one misquote escaped all seven verifiers in ROUNDS.md).
- **Coverage on the hit's own line.** A keyword hit or a blind-reader item counts as covered only by a row whose quote lies on that hit's line(s), or by a written verdict for that hit. One big row over a window no longer settles everything under it.
- **Dismissals are visible.** Every sweep verdict, reconcile decision, `info` mapping and rejected verifier finding is listed in a "What was set aside" section of the report with counts, so the operator sees what the agent decided was nothing.
- **Regression set split.** Misses the operator catches in a meeting are added to that meeting's `regressionSet` (with `recordingId`), and a re-run must catch them. Misses from past meetings become the agent's own fixture cases (transcript excerpt + expected item) that `eval.mjs` replays; they are not demanded of unrelated meetings.
- **Several recordings.** Every row, item, mark, extraction entry and regression entry carries `recordingId`; ids are namespaced (`P1:I12`). One ledger per recording, one item graph and one read per meeting; `run-check` checks across recordings.
- **Order of work** (one dependency order, replacing the package's numbering where it conflicted):
  1. `run-check --preflight`, before anything is fetched or written: the gate is on; the meeting's owner and audience pass the team checks (Team posture); the meeting folder will be inside `clientWorkspace`, its repository and remote match the declared `sync`, the local branch is pulled to the remote head, and the meeting lock is taken (item 4). The run then copies the helper scripts into `<attempt folder>/.helpers/` (git-ignored, never committed), hashes each copy and compares it with the sha256 manifest the build embedded in SKILL.md (`helperManifest`); a missing, extra or different file fails preflight. The run uses only those copies, so an update mid-run cannot mix versions, and the check is on the scripts' content, not on a version label two different files could share.
  2. Fetch → blind readers started → ledger and item graph → coverage, sweeps, reconcile and marks checks → meeting read → verifier rounds, each fix followed by a rebuild.
  3. Planned mapping check (against open items plus the planned staged set) → project-file updates and staging → actual mapping check against what was staged.
  4. Report written, then everything to be committed is staged.
  5. `run-check --final` on that exact staged snapshot: re-hashes the `.helpers/` copies against the manifest, re-runs every deterministic check itself (no reliance on earlier passes), checks the run record, scans every staged file for credentials (each hit needs the agent's verdict, item 3), and runs the containment scan (item 4).
  6. That snapshot committed (LOCK still held) → pushed → `run-check --after-push` confirms the remote has that exact commit → the agent-owned actions and the client's open asks are registered as commitments (`POST /commitments`, keyed `meetingId/itemId`, so a re-run updates rather than duplicates), which reach the agent's other machines through the commitment replica stream, not through the git remote → the evidence copy (transcript, marks read, ledger, meeting read, report) is written under `.instar/meeting-evidence/` and recorded as working-set artifacts, so a surviving machine can pull the read and its evidence without the remote (Multi-machine posture); the run is not `ready` until at least one other machine of the agent confirms it holds them: the run asks a live peer (from the pool's machine list) to pull the evidence through the working-set handoff, then reads back from that peer that the meeting's commitments (by key) and the evidence files (by hash) are present. Until a peer confirms, the run status is `ready-pending-replication` and the report says which machine has not confirmed; the confirmation is retried within the run's time budget, and a run that times out on it follows the timed-out-run rule below. On a single-machine agent the status says so: `ready (single machine: the meeting read and evidence are on this machine and the git remote only)` → notify (keyed `meetingId/revision`, receipt recorded) → a last small commit marks the LOCK `released` with the outcome and pushes it (`run-check --after-push` checks that this commit changes only the LOCK file) → the local lock is removed. Delivery receipts are written outside the checked snapshot. On any failure the local lock is removed and the git LOCK stays held with the failure recorded. A transient failure is retried within the run's time budget (`run.timeBudgetMinutes` in the client config, default 120, the same budget that bounds the whole run). Transient means a network failure, a rate limit, or temporary local contention (a git index lock held by another process, a busy file). The wait follows the signal: a rate limit's own reset time or `Retry-After` when the service gives one; otherwise exponential backoff with jitter, starting at about 30 s. Local contention gets at most five retries a few seconds apart, then is treated as a real failure. Before each retry the agent reads the remote to see whether the earlier attempt actually landed, so a push is never repeated on top of itself. When the next wait would run past the budget, the run stops and reports the failure with the LOCK held. It does not leave that to chance: before stopping it registers a follow-up commitment (`POST /commitments`, keyed `meetingId/resume`, due at the failing service's reset time when one was given, otherwise in one hour), which the commitment follow-through machinery brings back on its cadence. When it comes due the agent either resumes the run from the kept worktree (re-running `run-check --final` on that snapshot first) or closes it: marks the LOCK `released` with outcome `abandoned` and the reason, removes the worktree, the local lock and the evidence copy, closes the commitment, and tells the operator what was not done. After three resumes that fail the same way, it closes the run that way and says why. A later run of the same meeting closes the open follow-up as superseded. Anything else (refused credentials, a conflict, a failed check) is not retried; it is reported. Anything that is not a network failure is not retried; it is reported, and the next run takes the LOCK over under the rule in item 4.

### 3. Scripts (built-in helpers)

The package's scripts (`windows.mjs`, `build-ledger.mjs`, `coverage-check.mjs`, `sweeps.mjs`, `diff-extractions.mjs`, `mapping-check.mjs`, `marks-check.mjs`, shared `lib.mjs`) plus four new ones (`run-check.mjs`, `fresh-read.mjs`, `eval.mjs`, `convert-ledger-src.mjs`) and the build-generated `generated-constants.mjs` (item 1) become templates in `src/templates/scripts/meeting-transcript/`, installed to `.instar/scripts/meeting-transcript/` by `installMeetingTranscriptScripts(projectDir)` in `init.ts`, which iterates an explicit name list (mirroring `installBrowserScripts`) and is called from both `init` paths, `refreshScripts()` and `PostUpdateMigrator.migrateScripts()`. Always overwritten. Changes from the package:

- **No executed input.** The ledger source is JSON (`ledger-src.json`), read with `JSON.parse` and validated against a schema. Nothing the agent writes is imported or run by the checks.
- **Converter for older ledger sources (`convert-ledger-src.mjs`, new).** Agents that ran the package before this ships have `.mjs` ledger sources and re-run old meetings when a later call reverses something. The converter turns one `.mjs` source into `ledger-src.json` once, on the agent's explicit command, for a file inside a meeting folder: it loads the file in a separate Node process started with Node's permission model (`--permission`, read access to that one file only, no write, no child processes, no workers) and with physical ceilings: the parent samples the child's whole-process memory (RSS, not only the V8 heap) and kills it past the cap, plus a wall clock and a cap on captured output. The caps follow measured need and the host's capacity, not source size: the build converts every Luna-shaped fixture (including the largest real ledger source in her package) and records peak memory, time and output size; the default caps are four times those measured peaks, and the memory cap is never more than a quarter of the host's free memory at run time (read when the converter starts). Hitting a cap is a refusal the agent rules on, not a silent failure: the message names the cap that tripped, the measured use when it tripped, the host's free memory, and the cap to retry with. The agent may retry once with a higher cap within the host limit and records why; past the host limit it reports the ledger as not converted, with that message, and the old meeting is re-run from its transcript instead. It prints the exported data as JSON, and the parent validates it against the schema before writing. The output is the only thing the shipped checks ever read; the `.mjs` is never loaded by them.
- **Entry guard fixed.** The package's `import.meta.url === file://argv[1]` guard fails on a symlinked path or a path with a space, so the script prints nothing and exits 0. Every script runs its main when invoked directly by comparing `fileURLToPath(import.meta.url)` with `realpathSync(process.argv[1])`; a test runs each script through a symlink and a spaced path.
- **Exit codes.** 0 = check passed; 1 = check failed (with the list); 64 = bad input or usage (unreadable file, malformed transcript line, missing argument). Report-only modes (sweep listing, diff without `--ledger`) exit 0 and print `REPORT ONLY: not a check`; the skill never uses them as gates.
- **Paths stay in the meeting folder.** Every script takes `--root <meeting folder>` and refuses (64) an input or output path that resolves outside it, except the transcript and picture folder named in the client config. Hook paths in the config must resolve inside the agent home.
- **`marks-check` is a format check.** It checks that each mark has an entry with lines, items and a description in the reader's own words. Whether the description matches the picture is checked by a verifier given the pictures as image attachments (the adapters above). If the agent's framework has no image-capable adapter, the report says "screenshot facts were checked by the main reader only", the best status is `settled-with-limits`, and changes resting on mark-supported items are held (item 2a, Held evidence). The package's 60-character minimum becomes "a description, or `nothing` with a reason".
- **`run-check.mjs` (new).** Three modes (`--preflight`, `--final`, `--after-push`, see the order of work). In `--final` it re-runs every deterministic check itself and reads `run.json` and the run's files, and fails unless: each blind extraction and each verifier round file has a matching `fresh-read` receipt (prompt and reply hashes; for rounds, the prompt hash covers the read version reviewed) and carries the transcript's sha256 (receipts make a skipped reader visible; they cannot make fabrication impossible for an agent that writes both); each verifier round file has the schema (`finding, recordingId, lines, quote, kind: item-changing|firmness, held: bool, reason, reverses?`); the reported status matches the status table (item 2a) as computed from the LAST round; any finding whose final kind differs from the verifier's own label is listed as a downgrade in the set-aside section; every dispute is listed and its dependent changes held; each hook has a status (`ran`, `skipped-unconfigured`, `verified-absent`, `failed`); when staging ran, `liveUnchanged` is `true` from the hook's `verifyLive` before/after read (a hook-attested observation, not prevention; anything else makes the run `blocked`); the mandatory artifact set for the configured capabilities (item 5's matrix) is present; every hit of the credential patterns of Instar's durable secret scrub (from `generated-constants.mjs`, scanned in chunks so large vendor JSON is covered) on a staged file carries the agent's verdict in `run.json`: `credential` (the value was removed and the file re-staged) or `not-a-credential` with a reason. The pattern match is a signal, not the decision: it never fails a run by itself. What holds the push is a hit nobody has judged yet, because a pushed credential cannot be taken back; the agent, with the full context of the file, makes the call, and the report lists every hit and verdict; when staging ran, the raw before/after digests the hook returned are recorded in `run.json` and compared by `run-check` itself; the `.helpers/` copies still match the build's `helperManifest` hashes; and the meeting folder's git repository and remote match the client config's declared `sync` (Client data, item 5).
- **`fresh-read.mjs` (new).** Starts a fresh, tool-less reader: the prompt goes in on stdin, the reply comes back as JSON to a file in the meeting folder. On Claude Code it runs `claude -p --tools ""` with an empty allowed-tools list, `--strict-mcp-config`, a scratch working folder and an allowlisted environment, with pictures sent as image blocks over `--input-format stream-json`. On Codex it runs `codex exec -s read-only` from a scratch folder with `--disable shell_tool --disable unified_exec --disable apps --disable browser_use --disable computer_use -c mcp_servers={}` (the no-local-tools set Instar already uses in `CodexCliIntelligenceProvider.ts`), with pictures passed by `-i` (placed before the other arguments, the image list ended with `--`, so the stdin prompt is not read as an image path). The Claude stream-json call also needs `--output-format stream-json --verbose`. Both adapters carry the full argument and environment sets of Instar's existing tool-less providers, not only the no-tools flags: for Codex also `--skip-git-repo-check` and `-c project_doc_max_bytes=0` (without the first, `codex exec` refuses a scratch folder that is not a git repository); for Claude also `--setting-sources user` and `--allowedTools ''`, with an environment allowlist that keeps `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` and the XDG folders and adds the configured sign-in key the way `applyClaudeChildAuth` does, so the reader runs signed in. The sets come from `generated-constants.mjs`, which the build generates from the providers' own builders (item 1), so they cannot drift; the Gemini CLI and pi adapters are built the same way from `buildGeminiOneShotArgv`/`buildGeminiChildEnv` and `PiCliIntelligenceProvider`, plus their no-tools settings. The test-agent rung runs all four real CLIs on a fixture whose transcript asks the reader to read a file and run a command, expecting no tool use; an adapter that fails is reported as `no-fresh-reader` for that engine until fixed. Each call writes a receipt in the meeting folder (adapter, CLI version, provider and resolved model as the CLI reports them, outcome, latency, token usage where the CLI reports it, sha256 of the prompt including attached pictures, sha256 of the reply, time), and the exact prompt text and reply are kept beside the receipt in the attempt folder (client data, same privacy as the transcript), so a later review can see the full context each reader judged from, not only a hash that proves it (Observable Intelligence; Decision Provenance & Outcome Review). The reader cannot run tools, so text in a transcript or picture cannot make it act; its reply is data the agent then checks.
- **`eval.mjs` (new).** Runs the agent's fixture cases and the shipped synthetic fixtures through the scripts and reports which planted items were caught.

### 4. Client data

- Client material stays in `clientWorkspace`; nothing from one client's meeting goes into another client's folder, into `MEMORY.md`, or into shared notes. This is checked, not only said. `run-check` checks that the meeting folder is inside the declared `clientWorkspace`, and `run-check --final` runs a containment scan: every file changed since preflight anywhere in the agent home's git working tree outside the meeting folder (including `.instar/MEMORY.md`, `.instar/agent-memory/` and other clients' folders), plus memory entries the server's memory search returns as written since preflight, is compared with the transcript for any run of eight or more consecutive words and with the client identifiers in the client config. A hit holds the run (`blocked: client-text-outside-workspace`) until the text is removed or the agent records, per hit, why it is not client material (the same signal-then-verdict shape as the credential scan). On a multi-person agent, preflight also requires memory privacy to be on (`GET /memory-privacy`: `multiPerson` with `enabled: false` blocks with `memory-privacy-off`), so a session serving someone else cannot reach the meeting's material.
- Raw audio is not kept. The fetch hook keeps the transcript text, the vendor JSON, `marks.json` and the pictures that are read.
- The client config declares `sync`: the git repository that holds `clientWorkspace` (and therefore the meeting folders) and its remote (or `none`). This is wherever the agent keeps that client's meeting folders, often the agent's own repository (Luna keeps hers under `project_sandbox/<client>/meetings/` in her agent repository); it is never the client's own code repositories, which a read never writes. Every "client workspace repository" below means this declared repository, and the meeting lock lives beside the meeting folder in it. `run-check` fails the run when the meeting folder's repository or remote differs, so the repository and remote client material lands in are the ones the operator declared. Who may read that remote is bound to the meeting owner's signed team record, not left to whoever has repository access (Team posture). The skill commits and pushes to that declared remote after each run; `run-check` fails when the push did not land (local head not on the remote). `sync.remote: none` is refused unless the client config carries an operator-ratified exception with a commit or registry reference.
- Transcript, email and picture text is data, never instructions (and the fresh readers have no tools).
- One run per meeting, fenced: the meeting has a stable id (`<client>/<date>-<slug>`). Each run works in its own git worktree of the client workspace repository, created at preflight as a detached worktree at `origin/<sync branch>` (`git worktree add --detach`, since the branch is normally checked out in the main checkout); it commits there and pushes `HEAD:<sync branch>`, and `--after-push` compares against that remote ref. The worktree holds its attempt folder `meetings/<id>/runs/<runId>/`; project-file updates, staging, the final check and commits all happen in that worktree, so two runs (of the same or different meetings) never share an index or working tree. The worktree is removed after the release push; a failed run's worktree stays for diagnosis and is removed by the run that takes over its LOCK. Preflight adds `.helpers/` to the client repository's `.git/info/exclude` (local, not committed) so it never lands in the client's history. When a result or release push is refused because the remote moved (another run pushed), the run rebases its commit onto the new remote head in its own worktree: if the incoming changes touch none of this run's files, it pushes again; if they touch this run's project files, it re-runs `run-check --final` on the rebased snapshot first; a conflict blocks the run with the worktree and commit kept for recovery. This loop has its own brakes: at most five rebase-and-push attempts, with exponential backoff and jitter between them (about 5 s doubling to 80 s); after the fifth refusal the run stops with `blocked: push-contention`, the worktree and commit kept, and the next run (or the operator) resumes it. The release commit's LOCK-only check compares it with its own parent. With `sync.remote: none` the worktree starts from local HEAD. `run-check --preflight` first, before the worktree exists, takes a local exclusive lock (`O_EXCL` create of `<git common dir>/instar-meeting-locks/<id>`, shared by every worktree of that repository and never committed, which covers sessions sharing one checkout and `sync.remote: none`), then pulls and reads `meetings/<id>/LOCK`: an unreleased LOCK means held, and the run stops with `blocked: meeting-held`. Otherwise it commits a LOCK (run id, machine id, session name) and pushes; if the push is refused it pulls and checks again, so only an existing unreleased LOCK ever means held (a push refused because someone pushed another meeting's lock is retried, not treated as held). The run's last commit, after the notification, marks the LOCK `released` with the outcome, so a later re-run takes it without asking. The local lock is removed when the run ends, successfully or not; a local lock whose session is confirmed stopped (`GET /sessions`) may be removed by the next run. Before each side effect (`updateProjectFiles`, `stageChanges`, `notifyOperator`) the skill re-reads the remote LOCK and stops with `blocked: lock-lost` if it is no longer this run's. Staged changes are keyed `meetingId/itemId` and the notification `meetingId/revision` (the revision rises by one per completed run of that meeting), so a retry updates rather than duplicates and a re-run is announced as a new revision. A LOCK left by a run that never finished is taken over by the agent itself once the holding session is confirmed stopped (`GET /sessions` on its machine shows it gone, read from that machine); the takeover is recorded in `run.json` and listed in the report. If the holding machine cannot be read, the run stays blocked and the operator is asked once; no clock decides it.
- Nothing goes to the client: drafts only through the `drafts` hook; changes to client-visible surfaces only staged through `stageChanges`, whose independent live read must show no change (`run-check`); publishing waits for the operator's word; one message to the operator through `notifyOperator`, keyed by `meetingId/revision` so a retry does not send twice. The hook sends through the agent's own outbound messaging path, which checks at send time that this machine owns the destination conversation (a standing-down session's sends are refused); a refusal counts as a notify failure and is left to the owning machine's retry.

### 5. Hooks

One versioned hook contract in the appendix: `fetchTranscript`, `recorderExtras`, `recorderNotes`, `gatherContext`, `updateProjectFiles`, `stageChanges` (must also implement `verifyLive`), `notifyOperator`, `drafts`. Each has inputs, outputs and failure behaviour. Required for status `ready`: `fetchTranscript`. Any other hook may be unconfigured: it is skipped and named in the report, and the parts that depend on it are listed as not done (for example, without `stageChanges` nothing is staged and mapping to cards checks only against `openItems`). A failed fetch is `failed`, never "no marks"; `recorderExtras` writes `{fetched: true, marks: []}` only after checking the recorder, and `marks-check` refuses a bare `[]` or a missing file. Instar ships no hook implementations.

What each hook's presence means for the checks (the matrix `run-check` uses):

| Hook | Configured and ran | Configured, found nothing | Configured, failed | Not configured |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fetchTranscript` (required) | checks run | — | `blocked` | `blocked` |
| `recorderExtras` | `marks-check` required | `{fetched:true, marks:[]}`; `marks-check` passes | `blocked` | marks not checked; best status `settled-with-limits` |
| `recorderNotes` | comparison written | noted | listed as not done | listed as not done |
| `gatherContext` | prep/agenda/open items checked | noted | listed as not done | mapping to cards checks only planned items |
| `updateProjectFiles` | files and versions listed | noted | `ready-with-disputes`, failure listed | listed as not done |
| `stageChanges` + `verifyLive` | live digests compared | nothing staged | `blocked` | nothing staged; live check not applicable |
| `notifyOperator` | receipt recorded | — | left to the agent's normal outbound retry (keyed, so no duplicate), then listed | report only in the meeting folder; listed |
| `drafts` | drafts listed | — | listed | no client drafts made |

`sync.remote: none` (operator-ratified exception only) skips the pull, lock push and after-push check, and the report says the meeting is on one machine.

### 6. Rollout, versions and cost

- **Gate.** `meetingTranscript.enabled` in config, omitted = the development-agent gate (the standard dark-on-fleet pattern, added by `migrateConfig` as an absent key). When off, neither the skill nor the scripts are installed and nothing is written; `run-check --preflight` also refuses, so a leftover copy cannot run. The gate is checked on every install path: the four `installBuiltinSkills` call sites (which gain a config argument), `migrateBuiltinSkills`, `migrateBuiltinSkillRenderings`, `refreshScripts` and `migrateScripts`; the integration test covers each, and the gate gets a `DEV_GATED_FEATURES` entry whose justification says it gates only the install of an agent-invoked skill (nothing runs or spends on its own). Luna's agent sets it to `true` as the first real user (her operator is Justin; his approval of this spec covers that).
- **Awareness.** The skill's description is what an agent sees at the dev rung. The awareness entry (`src/data/awareness/meeting-transcript-analysis.md`) ships with the fleet step, in the small PR that flips the default, so the fleet is not told about a feature it does not have.
- **Versions.** SKILL.md declares `helperVersion`; every script prints its version with `--version`; `run-check` fails on a mismatch. A later change to the skill text ships with a migration for the built-in allowlist (Migration Parity item 5); scripts change only with a version bump.
- **Luna's copy.** Her `gci-post-call` keeps her client hooks and calls the shipped scripts; her own script copies retire. That change is in her agent home: Echo sends the change list on thread 1f805ef5 as part of dev-agent-live and checks her next run uses `.instar/scripts/meeting-transcript/` (run.json records the script path and version).
- **Cost.** A run is 2 blind readers plus at least 2 and usually 4-7 verifier rounds, each reading the full transcript and read: about 0.4M tokens for a two-hour call at four rounds and up to about 0.8M at the default ceiling, on the agent's configured model (cost scales with that model's price). Verifier rounds run one after another. The run stops with `INCOMPLETE (quota)` when the quota tracker is shedding load, and stops at the client config's `verifier.spendCeilingRounds` (default 7) with `INCOMPLETE (ceiling)`.

## Maturation plan

- **Held-out fixtures.** Besides the shipped fixtures, the build writes a small held-out set (at least three transcripts) whose expected items (owner, referent, qualifier, screenshot fact) are annotated through Codex on a GPT-family model, and evaluated with a non-GPT reader (the Claude adapter), so the annotator and the evaluated reader are different model families, from the transcript and its screenshots (recording which source supports each expected item), before the skill text is final, and are never shown to the skill text, its prompts or the person tuning them. `eval.mjs` compares the read's item graph from a full skill run with those annotations. The set is small; its size is part of the fleet decision.
- **test-agent-live:** a throwaway agent with the gate on runs the full skill on the shipped synthetic fixtures and the held-out set: two-speaker and multi-recording transcripts with planted misses (a direct ask inside small talk, a "this" whose referent sets the priority, a dropped qualifier, a screenshot-only fact, an owner bait) and corrupted artifacts (one big row per window, a stale extraction, a wrong-transcript hash, a hook that reports `liveUnchanged` with no live read). Pass: every planted item appears in the read with the right owner and referent, every corrupted artifact fails the named script, and a prompt-injection line in the transcript causes no tool call.
- **dev-agent-live:** development agents (gate default) and Luna (explicit flag). Luna's next recorded client call after the release runs through the shipped scripts and her hooks.
- **graduation criterion:** two consecutive real meetings by the dev rung where all checks and `run-check` pass, the operator's review of each read finds no missing or wrong action, owner, decision or priority (operator-found escapes = 0, recorded in `run.json` from the operator's reply), and the verifier result is recorded as a figure (rounds, item-changing counts per round). The count is by meetings, not days, because the dev rung's call schedule is not ours to set.
- **fail branch:** any operator-found escape resets the count, is added to that meeting's regression set and the agent's fixture cases, and is filed as a fix to the method (feedback queue) before the next count starts. After three escapes without graduating, the fleet step is dropped and the operator is told the method is staying at the dev rung, with the escapes listed.
- **fleet:** a separate small PR after graduation flips the default and adds the awareness entry.
- **dark-window:** dark on the fleet until graduation (the gate defaults to the development-agent gate); live on development agents and on Luna by explicit flag from the release. The fleet step is the only way out of the window, and the fail branch can close it for good.
- **ladder:** meetingTranscript

## Multi-machine posture

- Skill, scripts, awareness entry: shipped content, identical on every machine via the update path.
- Hooks and client configs live in the agent home. A fetch hook usually depends on a recorder login on one machine: machine-local-justification: physical-credential-locality (a recorder or meeting-platform sign-in lives on one machine's disk); on a machine without it, the hook returns `failed` and the run is `blocked` there, never a partial run.
- Meeting folders: unified. The replication path is the client workspace's git repository and its declared remote (`sync` in the client config), pulled before every run (preflight) and pushed at the end of every run; `run-check` refuses a run whose folder is not in that repository or whose push did not land. Raw audio is not kept, so no machine-local evidence remains.
- What the agent owes after a meeting does not depend on the git remote being reachable. The agent-owned actions and the client's open asks are registered as commitments (`POST /commitments`, keyed `meetingId/itemId`) after the push, each carrying the decision it came from, any disputed reading that touches it, and the supporting transcript quote, and commitments reach every machine of the agent through the commitment replica stream between machines. A machine that cannot reach the remote still knows what was promised, to whom and by when, and can answer coherently; it pulls the full read when the remote is back. The run is not `ready` until another machine has confirmed it holds those commitments and the evidence (order of work, step 6); on a single-machine agent the status says the read lives on one machine and the git remote.
- The evidence (transcript text, vendor JSON, marks and pictures that were read, the ledger, the meeting read and the report) also reaches a surviving machine without the git remote. The working-set handoff only serves files under the agent's `.instar/` folder, and meeting folders usually live elsewhere in the client workspace. So at the end of each run the skill writes an evidence copy of those files to `.instar/meeting-evidence/<meetingId>/<runId>/` (git-ignored, client data under the same rules; the containment scan treats it as part of the meeting folder) and records each file in the working-set artifact store (`WorkingSetArtifactManager`) under the conversation the run was started from. Another machine of the agent can then pull it from the machine that produced it through the existing working-set handoff (`POST /coherence/fetch-working-set` for that conversation: verified slices, never overwriting, queued durably until the producer is back). The copy is removed when its meeting folder is next pushed and confirmed by a later run, or by the store's own 30-day cleanup. The handoff's own refusals still apply: a file flagged as holding a credential is not sent, and the report says which files were refused. The git remote stays the primary path; the handoff is the path when the remote is down. When the working-set layer is dark on the agent (the route answers 503), the run says in its report that the evidence lives only in the git remote and on this machine, and a multi-machine agent's report lists that as a verification limit.
- Privacy is the same on every path: the handoff moves the meeting's files only between the agent's own machines, into the same client workspace path; it does not widen the audience (Team posture), and a session serving someone outside the audience cannot read them on the receiving machine either (memory privacy, required on multi-person agents).
- A run belongs to one session and holds the meeting lock (item 4); a second run of the same meeting, on any machine or in the same checkout, finds the lock held at preflight and stops before writing anything. Every run starts from the remote head (preflight pull) and ends with a push the after-push check confirms.
- `fresh-read.mjs` scratch folders are deleted after each read and hold nothing that must survive.

## Team posture

- **Owner.** Each meeting read has one owner: the person who owns the client relationship, named in the client config as a person key from the agent's team record (`owner`). On a one-person agent that is the operator, and nothing changes for them.
- **Private by default.** The meeting folder, transcript, pictures, read, receipts and staged changes are private to the owner. The operator notification goes to the owner only.
- **Sharing only through the owner's signed record.** The client config's `audience` lists who else may see the client's meeting material, and each entry must match a share the owner made in the signed team record (`GET /team/people`); preflight blocks with `audience-unverified` on any entry the record does not carry. The agent can narrow the audience (drop an entry, stop syncing) but never widen it.
- **The remote's readers are part of the audience.** Whoever can read the `sync` remote can read the meeting. So the client config declares the remote's readers as part of `audience`, preflight checks the remote is private where its host can say so (for GitHub, the repository's `private` flag), and on an agent with more than one person the meeting folders must live in a per-client remote, not the agent's shared home repository, unless the owner's signed record shares that client's material with everyone who can read the home repository. Preflight refuses otherwise (`audience-unverified`).
- **Conversations.** In a conversation with someone outside the audience, the agent does not reveal the meeting's content; memory privacy (required on multi-person agents, item 4) keeps that conversation's session away from the material, and the skill text says so.
- **Proof.** The test-agent rung includes a member and a guest who are outside a meeting's audience and must not see or act on it through any agent surface, and one who was given a share and can (Tests).

## Honest limits (accepted)

- The main agent reads the same client text with its ordinary tools; only the fresh readers are tool-less. Sandboxing the main session per task is a separate, larger Instar change (filed to the feedback queue); here the defences are the tool-less readers, the instruction that client text is data, preflight checks before processing, the lock re-read before each designated hook, and the final snapshot checks after staging and before push.
- `verifyLive` is the hook's own observation of the live surface. `run-check` compares its digests, but cannot prove a hook did not publish and revert.
- A matching repository and remote prove where client material goes. Who can read the remote is checked only as far as its host reports it (a private flag) and as the owner declared it in the signed record; an agent cannot list every reader of an arbitrary git host.
- The containment scan catches copied transcript wording and client identifiers in tracked files and memory; a paraphrase written outside git or the memory store is not caught.
- A key shown in a screenshot is not caught by the text credential scan.
- Another machine can answer follow-ups about what is owed from the replicated commitments, and gets the full read and its evidence through the working-set handoff when the git remote is unreachable (Multi-machine posture); only when both the remote and every machine holding the folder are unreachable does a question about the evidence wait, and the agent says so.
- Between the remote LOCK re-read and a hook's mutation there is a short window in which an operator-approved takeover could land; takeover requires the holding session to be stopped first, which closes it in practice.
- Receipts make a skipped or invented reader visible; an agent that writes both the receipt and the file could still fabricate both. Operator-found escapes are the backstop measure.

## Alternatives considered

- **Leave it in Luna's home.** Every other agent re-learns the misses, and fixes never reach copies.
- **A server route that runs the method.** The method is mostly reading and judgment; scripts the agent calls keep the mechanical part simple and inspectable, and a route would need its own model calls and spend controls.
- **Recorder converters as built-in hooks.** They depend on vendor pages and each operator's accounts; the hook contract says what a converter must output.
- **A fixed round cap as the stop rule (the package's 7).** Rejected: both recorded runs reached it without a clean round, so a fixed cap ends runs as `INCOMPLETE` for spend reasons while hiding that as a method rule. The ceiling stays, named as a spend ceiling.

## Side effects and rollback

- Agents with the gate on gain one skill (and its canonical and Codex copies) and thirteen files under `.instar/scripts/meeting-transcript/` (twelve scripts and the generated constants).
- Each completed run registers commitments for the agent-owned actions and the client's open asks (keyed, so re-runs update them), and writes an evidence copy under `.instar/meeting-evidence/` recorded as working-set artifacts of the conversation the run was started from.
- The four intelligence providers export their argument/environment builders (no behaviour change), and the build gains one generator step with a CI freshness check.
- Rollback: set `meetingTranscript.enabled: false` and revert the release. Already-installed files stay on disk and the skill may still be listed, but `run-check --preflight` refuses a run while the gate is off, and no awareness entry points at them; meeting folders are client data and are never touched.

## Frontloaded Decisions

- **Script location:** `.instar/scripts/meeting-transcript/`, framework-neutral.
- **Overwrite policy:** scripts always overwritten; SKILL.md install-if-missing, later changes by migration.
- **Ledger source format:** JSON only; older `.mjs` sources are converted once by the sandboxed converter (requested by Luna, the first user, 2026-11-17, thread 1f805ef5), never read by the checks.
- **Hooks ship empty;** the contract ships.
- **Gate:** dev-agent gate default; Luna by explicit flag; fleet flip in a later PR after graduation.
- **Raw audio:** not kept.
- **Meeting lock:** a LOCK file pushed to the client workspace repository (git's own push refusal is the fence); no pool registry needed.
- **Graduation:** two consecutive clean real meetings; three escapes without graduating ends the fleet step.
- **Helper integrity:** sha256 manifest embedded at build, checked on the copies at preflight and final.
- **Reader flags:** generated at build from the Instar provider builders (no runtime import of Instar's TypeScript, no hand-kept copy).
- **Stop rule and statuses:** evidence floors, no fixed minimum (a zero-new-finding round for `converged`; a round after every item-changing fix); a run that stops with open firmness findings is `INCOMPLETE (firmness open)`, run status `incomplete`; statuses `converged` (zero new findings), `settled-with-limits`, `INCOMPLETE` (firmness open, quota, or spend ceiling, default 7 rounds); always reported.
- **Frameworks:** tool-less reader adapters for all four supported engines (Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, instar-native) at ship, built from the providers' own builders; `no-fresh-reader` only for an engine whose adapter has not passed the injection test, never a run with unisolated readers.
- **Copied constants:** none by hand; `generated-constants.mjs` is generated at build from the canonical definitions and CI fails on a stale copy.
- **Credential and containment hits:** signals that need the agent's per-hit verdict; an unjudged hit holds the push.
- **Disputes:** the agent settles from the evidence ladder before asking; the operator is asked only what no record answers.
- **Unchecked evidence:** changes resting on unread marks are held; with marks unknown, two-source changes, or reviewer-confirmed unambiguous requests that are reversible and gated, may stage; absence is known only from the recorder's per-meeting metadata.
- **Team:** private to the owner by default; sharing and the remote's readers bound to the owner's signed team record; per-client remote on multi-person agents; memory privacy required on multi-person agents.
- **Timed-out runs:** a follow-up commitment resumes or closes them and releases the LOCK and worktree.
- **After-meeting durability:** `ready` waits for one other machine to confirm it holds the commitments and evidence (single-machine agents say so in the status); commitments carry what is owed to every machine, and the working-set handoff carries the evidence, both independent of the git remote; same audience on every path.
- **Luna by name (cheap-to-change-after; Justin may override).** The Maturation Path standard says eligibility at each rung comes from role, not name. Kept: Luna by explicit flag as a recorded exception, because Justin asked for her as the first user. Alternative: a "first-user" role an operator gives an agent in config, Luna its first holder. Switching is a config and wording change after build.
- **Who decides graduation (cheap-to-change-after; Justin may override).** The pipeline-completeness standard prefers recurring autonomous review to routine human approval. Kept: the operator's review of two real meetings is the graduation evidence, because only the operator knows what was really meant in his calls. Alternative: an autonomous reviewer judges graduation from `eval.mjs` and held-out results, and the operator is told. Switching changes only the graduation rule.
- **Sync:** pushing to the declared remote is required; `none` needs an operator-ratified exception.
