# Work Board cards actually appear

## Problem statement

Echo's Work Board is switched on and works, but it has never held a card. In code a card is made in three ways: an agent session decides to write one, the owner makes one from a message, or a project app asks for a version approval. No Echo session has ever written one; every session asks Justin in Telegram, in plans or through the Chief of Staff. Meanwhile the Chief of Staff marks 7 tracked projects "waiting on you" (live read, 2026-11-18 03:11Z: 4 moving, 7 waiting on you, 1 drifting, 9 unknown), and keeps them in its own note and the Needs-you list, never on the board. The approved front-doors spec (decision 6) kept every Needs-you item out of cards, so the board's "Waiting on you" lane stays empty while "Needs you" is full.

Justin approved the plan whose slice 2 is "cards that actually appear": the Chief of Staff opens a card when a project waits on him, sessions write a card instead of a chat question, a recommendation and "why you" become required on agent cards, and each need is seen once.

## Proposed design

Three pieces. Nothing here changes who may approve: only a person's tap approves (work-board-asks §4), and no card ever advances, finishes or pauses a Chief of Staff binding.

### 1. Chief of Staff wait cards

**Switch.** `workBoard.chiefOfStaffCards` (boolean). Omitted ⇒ the development-agent gate (on for Echo, off on the fleet), the same rule as `workBoard.enabled`. Off ⇒ no wait card is opened, edited or closed; existing cards stay as they are. No config migration is needed (omitted is the default).

**One card per project, deterministic id.** `cardId = "wc_" + sha256("cos-wait:" + conversationKey)[0..16]`. A retry or a lease change finds the same card.

**When the loop syncs.** At the end of every Chief of Staff tick (the tick runs only on the Chief of Staff lease holder), after the checks and the note, the loop calls `waitCards.sync(...)` with the fresh union it read for the note, its bound projects and their folded check states. Sync reads every confirmed binding from that union (`foldBindingState`), closed ones included, so a closed project is told apart from one the read simply does not hold. Sync is skipped on the grace tick (the first tick after another machine held the lease, chief-of-staff-topic §2.1), so the previous holder's records have a tick to replicate. A failure inside sync is caught and logged; it never fails the tick. The board is built earlier in boot than the Chief of Staff loop; sync reads it through a lazy accessor and does nothing while it is absent.

**What counts as a wait.** A bound, confirmed, not-closed project that the owner has not paused, that is not parked or breaker-tripped (those are their own Needs-you rows, `cos:parked:` / `cos:breaker:`, and stay so), and whose newest check verdict is `waiting-on-user`. Two kinds, decided as `derivedNeeds` decides them:
- *confirm*: every deterministic done item passes and at least one judgment item exists.
- *answer*: any other wait.

**The card's fields** (written by the server, never from a caller's body):
- `title`: "`<project name>` is waiting on you" (confirm: "`<project name>` looks done and needs your OK").
- `need`: the goal; the project's done checklist from the binding with each item's last state (passes / fails / not known; judgment items "needs your judgment"); labelled as the check's own words, the note of the check that opened (or reopened) the card; and "Waiting on you since <time>" (a person's reading of the time, always in UTC and named so, for example "Nov 17, 2026, 10:15 PM UTC", so every lease holder writes the same words), where the time is that of the Chief of Staff record that started this wait: the first `waiting-on-user` check after the newest record that ended the previous wait (a clearing record, an owner resume, an owner unpark, or, once a card was closed, the record its `mirror` was moved to at that close, since a park leaves no record of its own), or that boundary record itself when no waiting check has followed it yet. The opening note is the newest real note among the waiting checks since that start. No record id appears in the text (the wait's record stays in `mirror.waitRef` and the logs). When a new wait on a finished card would read word for word like the old one, the line reads "Waiting on you again since <time>" (alternating with the plain line), so every new wait on a finished card still changes the material. The note is the newest non-carried-forward check note (as `projectDetail` picks it), inserted as plain text with no backslash escapes: the sequences that would render a link, an image or an HTML tag are broken (`](` and `![` get a space, a `<` before a tag becomes a look-alike bracket), so it renders inert. Each checklist line reads "- <item> (<state>)". Alongside `need`, the card carries `brief` (§2 New fields), the same content as structured fields. It is the check-event's note exactly as recorded (that record already stores at most 500 characters, an existing limit of the Chief of Staff store); when it was cut there, the card says so and points to the project conversation, where the full context lives. The opening note and wait start are kept on the card (`mirror`, below), so later checks do not rewrite them. The composed text stays within the owner-set `need` and `title` limits (a write over a limit is refused, never cut, work-board-asks Fixed limits): when it would not fit, checklist items are dropped from the end with "and N more items on the project's own page", then the goal becomes "see the project conversation", then the note is replaced by a line saying it was too long to show; each checklist item's text is shown up to 200 characters, and a shortened item says so ("shortened; the full item is on the project's page").
- `recommendation` (deterministic): answer kind: "Reply in the project's conversation with your decision on what the check names; its next check picks it up." Confirm kind: "Check the items that need your judgment; if they hold, use Confirm done on the Chief of Staff. If something is missing, say what in the project's conversation."
- `whyYou` (new field, deterministic): answer kind: "The Chief of Staff marks a project waiting on you only when its next step needs your answer; it does not decide for you." Confirm kind: "Finishing a project you judge is your sign-off; the Chief of Staff checks the automatic items but never closes such a project itself."
- `doneWhen`: one item, `wait-cleared`, `agent-may-certify`: "The Chief of Staff no longer marks this project waiting on you." The binding's done items are shown in `need`, not used as the card's done items: the card closes when the wait clears, not when the project is finished, and nothing a card records may finish a binding (work-board-asks, One home).
- `carrier`: the team owner (`"owner"`, resolved by the service). `outward: false`. `projectKey`: the project's conversation key, so the card page links to the project conversation and the Work door's project row lists the card. `source` is not set: a source names a message, and a wait has none.
- `covers`: `["cos:waiting:<conversationKey>"]` (piece 3). The `cos:` namespace is reserved to these cards.

**Server-owned.** A card whose `covers` holds a `cos:` key is server-owned: the service refuses `409 server-owned-card` for every agent, session or person write of its fields (edit, waiting-on, evidence, claim) unless the writer is the in-process sync, which calls the service with an internal `{ kind: 'agent', server: true }` principal that no route can construct. A person's taps (hold, archive, reopen, confirm done, remove) work as on any card. The records themselves still say `by: agent`; server-ownership rests on the card's `covers`, which only that writer can set (on an older build, which drops `covers`, sessions could write to a wait card; named under Multi-machine posture).

**Fencing (HLC against HLC).** Each wait card carries `mirror`: the record key and HLC of the Chief of Staff record it last reflected, plus the wait start and the opening note. Sync acts on a card only when the project's newest Chief of Staff record (a check-event or a binding-lifecycle record: owner pause, owner resume, close, reopen, unpark) has a newer HLC than `mirror.hlc`. Both are HLCs from the same replicated clock: the comparison is against the recorded source version the card last reflected (causally later records always compare later; two concurrent records are ordered by the HLC's deterministic tie-break, identically on every machine). A person's tap never touches `mirror`, so it never delays a write. A holder whose Chief of Staff data is behind the card's `mirror` writes nothing. A holder missing both the newer Chief of Staff record and the card write that reflected it can write once from its older view; the next sync by an up-to-date holder corrects it, because that holder's newest record is newer than the stale `mirror`. No lease epoch exists to fence on; this is the bounded, self-correcting alternative, and a test covers it. An unchanged wait writes nothing (the composed text is the same; `mirror` is not rewritten alone).

**Create.** Only when the board index holds no record at all for that card id. Counted against `maxAgentCardsPerDay` (the fixed-id create path gains a `counted` option for this; project-app version cards stay uncounted as today). If the owner's open "Waiting on you" count is already at `softOpenCards`, the card is not opened this tick; the Chief of Staff's own Needs-you row still shows the wait, so nothing is hidden. A refusal is logged once per project and reason per process. A capacity refusal (soft cap, daily ceiling) is retried only once a newer Chief of Staff record exists for that project (its next check, on the project's own cadence), never every tick; a transient one (no owner wired yet during boot, the store briefly unavailable) is retried on the next tick, one attempt per tick. This brake is per process; a restart retries once. After a create the card's conversation is ensured (idempotent, derived key), and every later sync ensures it again for every existing wait card (an existence check), so a crash between the card and its conversation heals on the next tick as well as at boot (`WorkBoardRuntime.ensureConversations`). The `created` record key is fixed per card, so a second `created` written in a race before the first replicated replaces it under that key (newest wins, as today, in both the boot fold and the live apply). So that it can never sort after the card's later records and make them unreadable, **the fold takes a card's `created` record first, whatever its clock** (in `foldCard` and in the pickup-cause walk). The card's `createdAt` is then the later writer's time.

**Edit an open card.** When the composed `title`, `need`, `recommendation` or `whyYou` differs from the card's, one edit writes the change. The composed text holds no clock time and keeps the opening check's note (it is not re-read on every check), so it changes only when the wait kind, the checklist, an item's state or the goal changes.

**Close.** When the wait has cleared (a newer check verdict other than `waiting-on-user` and `unknown`, a recorded owner pause, a recorded binding close, or the project parked or breaker-tripped; an owner unpark ends that, and a later wait opens the card again): one evidence record on `wait-cleared` ("The Chief of Staff's check at <time> no longer marks it waiting on you (it reads <verdict in words>)", "You paused this project on <date>", "The project was closed on <date>", or "The Chief of Staff parked this project; its own line in Needs you asks you to unpark it"), then a claim. Both records, and the evidence text, derive from the first clearing record after the one `mirror` reflects (and, after an owner's reopen, after that reopen tap), plus the card's reopen generation, so a retry after a crash between the two, or a second holder, writes the same records with the same words; no claim is written while a current claim exists, and after an owner's reopen no claim is written until a clearing record newer than the reopen exists. Then `mirror` moves to the newest Chief of Staff record, marked `cleared` (a non-material edit, allowed on a finished card, voiding nothing, written once per close). So the fence holds again, a holder that lacks these records cannot read a later waiting check as a new wait, and later clearing checks write nothing. Which records clear is read as `foldBindingState` reads them: an owner pause only from an owner tap, once per ask (a redelivered callback is ignored) and only with its who, when, quote and message; a close only of the current binding. The card's text is not edited after its wait cleared. The item is `agent-may-certify`, so the card is done at once, labelled as the agent's own evidence. After an owner has reopened the card once, every later finish needs the owner's Confirm done (work-board-asks §5): the card then sits in "claims done" in Waiting on you until the owner confirms or archives it.

**Reopen (a new wait).** A new wait is a wait after the card has a `cleared` mirror, a wait after any clearing record newer than `mirror` (a wait that cleared and came back between syncs), or a wait whose start differs from the one the card reflects. A card the person finished themselves (Confirm done) while the same wait continues stays finished and is not rewritten; only a new wait reopens it. It writes the new material with the new wait start and note: a server-owned rewrite (the existing `serverOwnedRewrite` path, used by project-app version cards) for a finished card. The new wait start (or, at the same minute with the same words, the "again" line) changes the material, which voids the old claim and finish (both are bound to the material hash), so an old Confirm done can never finish the new wait. An owner resume after a pause is a new record; if the newest check still says waiting, the card reopens with it, as the Chief of Staff's own row does.

**Repair.** One case passes the fence: a card closed on a clearing record (`cleared` mirror) that now reads open with no claim (a competing `created` with other material, written in a lease race, voided its claim). Sync closes it again on a clearing record after the wait's start; after an owner's reopen it still waits for a clearing record newer than the reopen.

**Never on ignorance.** A newest verdict of `unknown`, or a project absent from the union the tick read, leaves the card as it is. Absence is not evidence.

**A person's word wins.** Sync never edits, closes or reopens a card that is held, archived or removed. An archived wait card stays archived when the project waits again: the Chief of Staff's own Needs-you row shows that wait, and the owner can restore the card.

**What a person does with it.** The card sits in "Waiting on you" with no Approve step (piece 2). It opens to the project conversation through its project link. The owner answers there, taps Confirm done on the Chief of Staff, or pauses the project; the next check clears the wait and the card closes itself. No answer is ever taken from a card into a binding.

**Not judged by the spec-approval check.** Route writes of agent cards pass the B25 spec-approval judge. Wait cards are written in-process and do not: each mirrors a need the Chief of Staff already raises in its note and in Needs-you, and adds no ask of its own. If B25 is needed for these waits, it belongs in the Chief of Staff's wait verdict, which feeds both.

**Older states.** When the newest verdict becomes `unknown` for a long run, the Chief of Staff's own row leaves Needs you while the card stays (it is never closed on ignorance), so the card row shows again in Needs you. That is the last recorded state, shown as the card's own words.

**Measurement.** Server-owned cards are left out of the board's graduation metrics and the pilot start (work-board-asks, Maturation plan): their mechanical closes would otherwise dominate "agent-attested finishes" and the reopen share. They are counted separately (`serverOwnedCards`) in the same metrics record.

### 2. Cards instead of chat asks; recommendation and "why you" required

**Required fields.** `createCard` by the agent principal through the route refuses `400 recommendation-required` or `400 why-you-required` unless both are non-empty text; an agent edit that clears either on an agent-created card is refused the same way. These are presence checks, signals of a real ask, not judges of its quality. In-process server cards: wait cards always carry both; project-app version cards are exempt (their recommendation would only restate the approve button). Person-written cards are unaffected.

**New fields on the card.** `whyYou` (text or null; not material; same limit and credential check as `recommendation`), `covers` (a list of Needs-you item keys; not material; bounded only by the record-size ceiling, like done items; each key `^[a-z][a-z0-9-]*:[A-Za-z0-9_.:#@-]{1,200}$`; which keys a caller may write is fixed, piece 3) and `mirror` (server writer only, refused from any other writer: `{ref, hlc, waitSince?, waitRef?, note?, cleared?}` — the reflected record and its HLC, the wait's start time and record, the opening note, and whether the card was closed on a clearing record; §1 Fencing). Replicated with the card like every field. `brief` (server writer only, refused from any other writer; not material, so writing or changing it voids no approval, evidence or finish; null clears it): `{ask, goal?, checks?: [{text, status: passes | needs-you | unknown | fails}], chiefSaid?, waitingSince?}` — one plain sentence of what is asked (the decision the check's note names, as in "Choose whether to run all three experiments, run only the guard test, or wait.", else "Confirm this project is done." or "Answer what the project needs next."), the goal, the done-when items with their state, the check's own words, and the wait's start as an ISO time for the client to format. All plain text. A wait card written before `brief` existed gets it on the first sync after a newer Chief of Staff record (on a finished card as a brief-only, non-material write). The card read (`cardJson`) carries `whyYou`, `covers`, `createdAt`, `serverOwned: true` and `brief` (absent when the card has none) on a wait card; apps should show `whyYou` under the recommendation, and may lay a wait card out from `brief` instead of `need`. An app that does not know them ignores them; an older machine drops `brief` and keeps the record.

**When approval is required: an amendment (the narrowest part of the approved rule C).** Today, under `every-agent-card`, a card the agent wrote waits for an Approve tap even when the agent carries nothing. Approval exists to let the agent *work* a card (`workable` requires `carrier: agent`); on a card a person carries there is no agent work for it to cover, so the owner is asked to approve a request to themselves before acting on it. The amendment: **a card that is not `outward`, whose carrier is a person, and that has no outstanding withdrawal, needs no approve tap.** A material edit moving the carrier to the agent restores the existing rule before any agent work. `outward` cards keep their own approve tap under every policy. This is the part of Justin's rule C (work-front-doors "Decision for Justin", approved as "approved, C") that needs no trust-level machinery: a card inside the agent's role (it asks the owner and does nothing) proceeds without a new yes. It is not self-approval: nothing is approved, and no work can start on such a card.

**Visibility floor, in the fold.** Approval also gated whether an agent's claim counts (a claim on an unapproved card never counted). So that removing the approve tap never lets the agent open, or edit, and then finish an ask the person never saw, the fold says: **an agent's claim on a card that is not `outward` and is carried by a person always needs that person's Confirm done**, whatever its items say and whoever wrote the card (a person-written card with an `agent-may-certify` item included). The one exception is a server-owned wait card, whose single item rests on a recorded Chief of Staff verdict, pause or close. `outward` cards are unaffected (they still need their own approve, and an approved claim counts as today; this keeps project-app version cards closing on their phone approval). work-board-asks §4 "the agent may never finish a card it was not approved to work" is amended to "...a card it carries and was not approved to work; on a card a person carries and that sends nothing outward its claim is finished only by that person's Confirm done, except a server-owned card mirroring a recorded source verdict".

**Existing cards.** The fold changes apply to cards already in the store: an agent-written, person-carried, inward card that waited for Approve now shows as the person's to do, and an agent claim on such a card (or on a person-written one with an `agent-may-certify` item) now waits for the person's Confirm done instead of finishing. Echo has none (the board has never held a card); other agents' boards are the fleet decision of the maturation plan (the board is dark on the fleet).

**Agent awareness.** One awareness entry (`src/data/awareness/work-board-cards-instead-of-asking.md`): when a session needs the owner's decision, answer or sign-off on work, it writes a card instead of asking in a message, with `recommendation`, `whyYou`, `doneWhen`, `projectKey` for a tracked project, and `covers` when the card is about a plan or a pull request already in Needs-you; then it says in chat, in one line, that the card is waiting. A session in a memory-scoped place cannot create cards (work-board-asks §4); it is told so and asks in its conversation as before. `carrier: "owner"` with `outward: false` is the shape for "only you can do this"; `outward: true` keeps its approve step. Permissions, secrets, promises and Chief of Staff sign-offs keep their own homes. When the decision is the one a tracked project is waiting on, the Chief of Staff's wait card already asks it: the session answers in that card's conversation or the project's, and writes no second card. Entries are rebuilt into every agent's instructions on update, so no template edit or migration step is needed.

### 3. Each need once: a covered row links to its card

Decision 6 of the front-doors spec stays: promises, authorizations, passwords, Chief of Staff completion and blockers keep their own lifecycles and are never converted into cards, and neither are plans awaiting an OK or feedback fixes awaiting a look (each has its own approval path; a card for them would be a second place to say yes).

What changes is how a card about one of them is shown. In `surfacedNeeds` (`dashboard/console/needs-model.js`, shared by the console's Home and the server's `GET /needs`), after every source's rows are built, a pure pass (`linkCoveredRows`, which returns new rows and never changes the cached rows the grace path replays): for each work-board row in the owner's shown "Waiting on you" slice (`workBoardNeedRows` shows at most `softOpenCards`; a card past that sits in "N more cards" and links nothing) whose card only waits on the person to act (status `with-person`: no Approve, no Confirm done, no open question, not `outward`), each other row whose key (without its `need:` prefix) is in the card's `covers` gains `card: { id, href, label }` with the fixed label "Has a card on the work board" (never the agent's title). Only a server-owned card (the Chief of Staff's mirror of a row it already raises) then drops its own row; an agent session's card keeps its own row as well, so no agent-written ask can be made less visible by covering another item (second-pass review). The covered row keeps its own words, its source and its own action, so nothing a source says is replaced by agent text, and its authorization path is untouched. A card that asks for Approve, an answer, a Confirm done, or something outward always keeps its own row, so an ask can never be tucked behind another item. (The plans source keys its rows `plan:plan:<id>`, its model's key already carrying `plan:`; linking reads that as `plan:<id>`, the key a card names. Existing row ids are unchanged.) When two cards cover one row, the older card (creation time, then id) links it and the other keeps its own row. A card none of whose covered rows is present keeps its own row. `GET /needs` items gain the optional `card` field (additive; `schemaVersion` stays 1 because no field changes meaning); an app that does not know it shows the row exactly as today.

**Which rows a card may cover** (a fixed list; every other key is refused `400 covers-not-allowed` at write): `cos:waiting:` (server-owned wait cards only), `plan:` (an initiative id) and `pr:` (a pull request number). Excluded on purpose: health, accounts, permissions, passwords, spending, context and attention rows (security, money and credentials), promises (decision 6), learned-skill rows (their verdict is the owner's own tap), and every source whose key is a list position rather than an item id (`feedback:…:<i>`, `secret:<i>`, `agent:…:<i>`), which could link the wrong item after a reorder.

So "each item once" holds exactly for the Chief of Staff's waits; a plan or pull request with a session's card shows its row (linked) and the card's row. Echo's 7 waiting projects show as 7 Chief of Staff rows in Needs-you, each linked to its card, not 14 rows; the board's "Waiting on you" lane holds the 7 cards. The Chief of Staff's own note keeps its lines (it is the Telegram summary, not a list surface).

**Amendment, 2026-11-18: one row per Chief of Staff wait.** Approved by Justin (topic 134088, 18:00 PST, CMT-1983): "Needs you" is the one list, and a project waiting on him appears once and opens its card. A row covered by a server-owned card on a key in the server-only `cos:` namespace now becomes that card: it keeps its key and source, takes the card's title and words, and its `action` opens the card. This applies whatever the card asks (an open wait or a Confirm done). The source's own link to the project conversation moves to an optional `alsoOpens` on the `GET /needs` item (`{ href, label: "Open the project conversation" }`, the same href rule as an action), and `card.label` reads "Opens its card on the work board". Both texts are the server's, and only the server writes `cos:` covers, so no agent text replaces a source's words. Every other covering card is unchanged: a covered plan or pull-request row keeps its own words and action and only gains `card`, and an agent session's card keeps its own row, so an agent's card still cannot take over another source's button. A client that does not know `alsoOpens` opens the card, and the card's text names the project conversation.

## Fixed limits

The 500-character note is the Chief of Staff store's existing check-event limit, not new here, and is disclosed on the card when hit. `covers` has no count limit of its own (the existing record ceiling bounds it). No other new limit.

## Multi-machine posture

- Wait cards: replicated (the `work-card-record` journal, unchanged). The writer is the Chief of Staff lease holder, fenced by the source record it mirrors, and skipped on the grace tick after a holder change.
- `whyYou`, `covers` and `mirror`: replicated with the card. A machine on an older build drops all three when it folds, so it shows the card without them and does not link covered rows: the owner sees a need twice there (once as the row, once as the card) until that machine updates. No action is needed; the update clears it. Without `covers` an older build also does not treat the card as server-owned, so a session there could write to it; the next sync on a current holder reasserts the text.
- The approval amendment, the person-carried claim rule and the `created`-first rule are fold changes. An older machine folds a wait card as needing approval and does not count its unapproved claim, so it shows open and closed wait cards in Waiting on you, asking for Approve, until it updates. That is a visible stale need, not a hidden one.
- Server ownership and the person-carried claim rule are enforced where records are written (the service) and in the new fold. An older build's agent could still edit or claim a wait card, and an older fold finishes an agent claim on an owner-carried inward card without Confirm done, so builds can disagree on whether such a card is done until every machine updates. The bound: only agent records are possible there; no approval and no outward path opens.
- An older build holding the Chief of Staff lease never syncs, so open wait cards stay open after their waits clear until a current build holds the lease. Named, not fixed here.
- The "logged once" memory for refusals is machine-local (logging only).
- Needs-you linking: computed per read from the replicated work-board and source reads; no new state.
- One-machine install: everything runs locally; no peer.

## Frontloaded Decisions

1. One wait card per project (deterministic id), reopened by rewrite, not one per wait episode: an episode id would depend on how much history the tick's union holds. The wait start shown is the time of the record that started the current wait, kept on the card.
2. The card's done item is "the Chief of Staff no longer marks it waiting", not the binding's checklist; the checklist is shown in `need`.
3. Plans, feedback fixes and promises are not auto-converted to cards. A card about a plan or pull request links its row; feedback and promises are not linkable in this slice.
4. Needs-you keeps the source row and drops the card row (not the reverse), so every source keeps its own words and action on every client, including ones that do not know about cards. (Amended 2026-11-18 for the Chief of Staff's own waits only: that row takes the server-owned card's words and opens the card, with the project conversation in `alsoOpens`; see §3, "one row per Chief of Staff wait".)
5. The approval amendment is limited to non-outward, person-carried cards, and an agent's claim on them needs the person's Confirm done (except server-owned wait cards).
6. Project-app version cards stay exempt from the required fields and stay uncounted against the daily ceiling; wait cards are counted.
7. Wait cards send no notification of their own, so night hours are untouched.
8. An archived wait card is not reopened by a later wait.
9. The switch is `workBoard.chiefOfStaffCards`, dev-gated like the board.
10. Person edits of a wait card's fields are refused (the card follows the project); taps work.
11. The Needs-you answer's `schemaVersion` stays 1 (the `card` field is additive).

## Rollback

Turning `workBoard.chiefOfStaffCards` off stops all wait-card writes and keeps everything else. A code revert below this version is not clean and is stated so: the old fold shows every past wait card as needing Approve in Waiting on you (the owner archives them, one tap each), and covered rows stop linking. So the switch, not a revert, is the rollback for the writer.
