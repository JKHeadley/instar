# In plain words

Right now, any device you've paired with your agent can make itself able to approve risky things, like permission requests or passwords. On a Windows PC, any program you run could quietly do the same and approve its own requests.

This step changes who can approve. Being able to approve becomes an entry in the signed ownership ledger your computers share, and only your phone can add itself to it. The very first time, your agent sends a code to your private chat and you type it on your phone. After that, a phone that can approve can let another of your devices approve too, or take that right away, each with Face ID.

Devices that can approve today keep working exactly as they do now, and you get a list of them so you can spot one you don't recognise. To join the ledger, one of them uses the private-chat code, or a phone already in the ledger lets it in. This step is the server side; the phone apps catch up in the next step.
