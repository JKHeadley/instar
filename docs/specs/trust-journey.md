## Problem statement

Justin asked how well Instar supports an agent that starts at its person's comfort level and earns more access by proving itself. The constitution already says who holds the keys: starting trust is the person's choice, the person can widen or narrow it at any time, and the agent never widens its own authority. What is missing is the journey between those points:

- Nothing the person sees shows how the agent has done.
- Offers to stop asking run on timers rather than evidence.
- A mistake does nothing predictable.
- The agent's own access token can raise its trust, or switch off the gate, on its own (G1).

This spec builds the journey from parts 1.x already has, and closes those holes first.

## Threat model

This spec closes the trust-widening routes the agent reaches through the server: its bearer token (including `PATCH /config` for the gate and evolution, and Slack role registration), the agent-readable PIN, agent-written approval, outcome and correction rows, unverified ingress forwards, and accidental or hallucinated raises. After stage 0, no server route widens the trust settings, board policy, gate config or evolution autonomy this spec governs without a person's approval through the account path in decision 5. The PIN arms that issue Slack floor grants through mandates (`/authorization-requests/:id/approve`, `/mandate/issue`, `/mandate/:id/grants`, `routes.ts:16084-16498`) predate this spec and belong to the mandate system; they are reported separately (feedback fb-f55d218a-a45) and are not claimed closed here.

It does not protect against an agent that edits its own disk or server code, or reads or edits its key files or the lifeline secret (a readable 0600 file, like the PIN; what differs is the disclosure, not custody). No software on a host the agent administers can; the Purpose's signed path needs a custodian the agent cannot administer, which the approval-custody work will provide. Until then the Purpose's account exception is the path, and it is honest by construction: its disclosure says the agent can also use the account and that the account does not show who approved. This spec never labels any evidence "verified" or "signed" as authority. When the custody work lands, `OperatorProof` takes its proof kind with no other change here, and a phone-based approval can then become an authority path.

## Frontloaded Decisions

1. **No new trust engine.** `AdaptiveTrust` stays the authority for settings, `ApprovalLedger` for approval decisions, `TrustRecovery` for incidents, the `AuthorizationRequestStore` machinery (hashing, dedup, server-rendered card) for offers, the work board for cards. `AutonomyProfileManager` is not used to set per-kind settings.
2. **A kind is `service.operation`**, the existing `(service, OperationMutability)` key, for write, modify and delete. The same string is the `ApprovalLedger.decisionClass`. Reads are not kinds in this spec: no read grant is imported, offered or shown, and "What I may do alone" says "reading is not gated". The hook's name-based read fast path (`PostUpdateMigrator.ts:24310-24330`) treats names like `search_and_reply` as reads, so for a service that has any governed kind a call is a read only when its exact tool name is an entry marked read in `effect-kinds.ts` (the hook migration writes those names into the hook as a generated list); every other call on such a service goes to the server, and a name the table does not match asks. Services with only unchanged legacy kinds keep today's fast path. The kind, with its account and audience, is the authority floor: it says what an action physically does. It is not how the agent recognizes a repeated task; from stage 3 that is judged by meaning (decision 14), which can only narrow the floor.
3. **Authority is a scoped grant.** Each grant is `{grantId, person, kind, account, audience, conditions, onMistake, level, proofRef, revision, setAt, setBy}`, plus from stage 3 an optional `category` (section 5a) that narrows it to one kind of task and a `scope` list when one task spans several kinds; `account` and `audience` may be "any" only on legacy grants (decision 9). Grants live in a new sibling field `grants[]`; `operations[op]` stays a `TrustEntry` holding the effective level, written as the most restrictive matching grant, so a build that predates this spec never reads a level wider than this build would. `trust-profile.json` becomes a projection of the replicated grant records. The effective level for an action is the most restrictive matching grant, intersected with the owner's ceiling and with each affected person's restriction. An action no grant covers is at "Ask me first". From stage 3, one person's grants combine by specificity, then restriction: a category grant applies to actions it fits, and for those actions it replaces each of that person's kind-level grants that matches the action only when each such grant is absent or is a bundle "Ask me first" recorded before the category grant's current revision was approved (the card names it). A line the person changed in the comfort conversation is recorded `setBy: person` and is a restriction. A `requireApproval` entry, a legacy or person-set ask, and an "Ask me first" recorded after that approval (by the person or an `onMistake` drop) are restrictions and apply as most restrictive. A person-set "Don't do this", a narrowing record, the owner's ceiling and every affected person's restriction still apply as most restrictive. An action that does not fit falls to the kind-level grant. `operations[op]` stays the most restrictive grant, for older readers; a category grant is never written into it, so its kind projects as "Ask me first".
4. **Raising needs an `OperatorProof`; a person narrowing does not.** Raising is any change that makes effective authority less restrictive: the level, account, audience or conditions (including `onMistake`); the floor dials (the money amount, sensitive marks); a board policy change to anything but `every-agent-card`; a profile change whose effective permissions (agent autonomy, safety, autonomous evolution, any trust level) are wider after than before; or a config change that disables the external-operation gate, removes or loosens a blocked or read-only service, a `requireApproval` entry or a service permission, or widens `evolution` autonomy. `PATCH /config` refuses those without an `OperatorProof` and keeps accepting changes toward more restriction; `trustJourney` is never added to `PATCHABLE_CONFIG_KEYS`. Registering or approving a Slack principal as `owner` or `admin` is also a raise. A dashboard feature toggle whose action is a raise (for example the gate switch, `FeatureDefinitions.ts:250`) opens the matching decision-5 request instead of calling `PATCH /config`. A bearer-only or PIN-only raise returns 403 and names the approval it needs.
5. **The approval path is the account path, with its disclosure.**
   - **Who approves what.** The owner approves anything, including the role ceiling, board policy and installation-wide changes. A member approves only changes to their own things, within the owner's ceiling. Guests approve nothing. People are resolved through the owner's signed team record (`teamRoutes.ts`; the bearer can only propose or remove). That record identifies people but is not custody; it is safe here because every approval still needs the person's own authenticated direct conversation and acceptance. The owner's platform ids are read only from config values no server route writes (`messaging[telegram].config` owner user id; `mobilePairing.slackOwnerUserId`), never from `SlackPrincipalResolver`, `UserManager` roles, `authorizedUserIds` or topic-operator bindings; changing either value is a raise. When an adapter's owner id is unset, that adapter has no approver: the verified-operator fallback (`TelegramAdapter.ts:2382-2409`) is never used for this path, a raise or ask through it is refused naming the missing setting. An unset owner id is established through the existing installation setup conversation (the setup wizard, which runs in chat), never by a direct message alone; a platform receipt, messaging access or a shared-access acceptance never enrolls an owner. "What I may do alone" names the missing binding and offers that setup step; later changes remain raises, and `PATCH /config` refuses both owner-id keys.
   - **What counts.** The approving person's reply `approve <code>` in their 1:1 direct conversation on a configured adapter whose sender id the platform authenticates: a Telegram private chat whose chat id equals that person's Telegram user id (never a topic id), or a Slack DM from that person's id in the configured workspace. The reply must be a platform reply to the server-sent request card's message id. The code is six characters (case-insensitive base32), unique per installation, one per card. A card and its code expire after `trustJourney.cardTtlDays` (default 7, config). Basis: the Purpose consumes an approval only against an "unexpired" request, and the expiry bounds how stale a rendered card can be; it costs the person nothing, because a reply to an expired card gets a fresh card with a new code for the same open offer, which itself stays open (section 5). The code binds a reply to a card; it is not a secret (it appears in message history), and knowing it forges nothing.
   - **Ingress.** A reply counts only as the adapter's own platform receipt. Telegram: the server's own poll, or a lifeline forward whose ingress decision is `verified: true` from the lifeline itself (not `server-self`, not bypassed, never `senderTrusted` alone), with `senderIsBot` false, no forward origin, chat type `private`, a `replyToMessageId`, and the `update_id`, which the lifeline now forwards and which is recorded and single-use. Slack: only an event that reached `SlackAdapter._handleEvent` from the Socket Mode callback or the history-recovery replay, keyed single-use by (channel, ts). Mobile inbound (including phone-to-Slack dispatch), lifeline replays after an outage (which carry none of these fields; the person is asked to reply again), mesh or durable-queue redelivery and every other internal route never count. The receiving machine writes a receipt record (platform ids, message id, chat id, proposal hash); the holder consumes it, and the receiver is identified by the record's journal-envelope origin matched against that machine's advertised adapter role, never by a request header.
   - **Acceptance.** Each approving person records their own acceptance per adapter: the server sends, in that direct conversation, "I accept that my replies from <service> account <id> count as my approval for Instar installation <id>, even though my agent can also use this account. I can withdraw this at any time."; the person sends it back unchanged; it is stored with the principal and message reference and acknowledged there. One person's acceptance never stands in for another's. Withdrawal is a narrowing, accepted in the person's own words from any conversation, the dashboard or a phone. It stops new consumption only; grants already committed stay (section 6). Neither an acceptance nor a grant enrolls a person or changes team access.
   - **Disclosure.** Every approval through this path is shown, in records, displays and exports, as "approved from your account, which your agent can also use; this does not prove who approved".
   - **Phones.** A presence signature from an owner phone over the same proposal hash may be added and shown as "also confirmed on a phone paired with your agent". It is evidence, never authority for a raise, until the custody work supplies a custodian (Threat model). A dashboard PIN constructs nothing.
6. **An offer may propose any level for the same scope, including "Just do it".** The person's approval is the floor, not a level cap: `MAX_AUTO_LEVEL` keeps capping what `checkElevation` suggests on its own, but no longer limits what the agent may offer with the record attached. The agent judges how far the record supports (decision 11); an offer above "Do it and show me" says that the person will no longer see each action. Only a scope enlargement is a bigger step.
7. **The record is counts over a period, never a score.** The `maturity` percentage leaves every person-facing summary.
8. **The comfort answer writes per-kind grants only**, from a static bundle table (section 3), never through `setProfile`.
9. **Existing agents keep exactly what they have today.** At stage 0, for each concrete kind (a service named in the gate's config, seen in the operation log, or configured as an MCP server for this agent on that machine at import time in the project and user Claude MCP config or the Codex config, times each operation), the import records a legacy grant for the owner whose policy is today's gate behaviour for that kind, computed purely from the inputs the live gate actually uses on that machine (G4: `agentAutonomy.level` through `AUTONOMY_PROFILES`, `externalOperations.services[s].permissions/blocked/requireApproval`, `readOnlyServices`, and the bulk rule) before conversion to an action, so `proceed` and `log` stay distinct, as a function of reversibility and item count, so a simple operation never authorizes a riskier variant. Gate config is already per machine today (existing state this spec does not change), so each legacy grant records one machine's behaviour in a unified, replicated record and applies on that machine; confirming or replacing it produces one grant that applies everywhere; where `externalOperations.enabled` is false, today's observed result is that every non-read MCP effect is refused (the route returns 404 and the hook exits 2, `routes.ts:36038-36040`, `PostUpdateMigrator.ts:24435-24440`), so the legacy grant records `blocked`. `migrateConfig` writes `trustJourney.legacyImportedAt` per machine when the import commits; with that marker present and the stores missing, the machine takes the recovery path and never imports again. A machine that joins after stage 0 imports a legacy grant only for a kind that already holds an unchanged legacy grant on some machine, capped at the narrowest of those grants; it never copies another machine's. Every other kind on the joining machine is governed: it follows the existing grants, or asks if there are none. It makes no LLM call, writes nothing to the operation log, and does not call `evaluate`. Stored `AdaptiveTrust` levels were never enforced (G4): they are kept as historical settings and never become new restrictions or permissions. A wildcard is never imported. Legacy grants are labelled "set before approval was required, not confirmed by you", can be narrowed, confirmed or replaced by an approved change but never widened (a person's narrowing replaces the legacy grant for that kind on every machine with a person-set grant labelled "set by you", which is then a governed kind), never count as proof, and never serve as a template for a new kind. The list is shown to the owner once in "What I may do alone". Internal system services (`parity-sentinel`) are not person kinds: they move to a system-owned allowlist (a code constant in `src/data/`, never config, listing only Instar internal services), their seeded entries are dropped from the import, and the migrator's write is retired. Existing board policy is kept until the person answers the comfort question. Justin's message 142440/143258 authorizes preparing Echo's move to option C; the move itself goes through decision 5 as an exact request.
10. **New installs and unused kinds grant nothing.** `DEFAULT_TRUST` is not used for any kind without a recorded grant: such a kind is at "Ask me first", and the comfort question stays visible in "What I may do alone" until answered. Balanced is shown as the suggested answer, never applied. Basis: the Purpose makes starting trust "the person's choice", and "access is not permission".
11. **When an offer is made (Judgment Within Floors).** Floor: at least one checked outcome under the offered category (for earn-back, under the restored grant), and the record attached showing every correction and undo in its period and the one behind its most recent drop. A correction or undo weighs heavily but is not an automatic bar; an offer made despite one says what went wrong and why later evidence outweighs it. Above the floor the agent judges. The `DEFAULT_ELIGIBILITY` numbers (5 asks, 90% unchanged, streak of 5) and earn-back's 10 become default guidance in the operator policy register; `checkElevation`'s threshold and `autoApprovalEligible` no longer refuse a well-supported offer. Fallback: no offer. Cheap-to-change-after: the guidance numbers are config, and an offer grants nothing until approved.
12. **Offers are standing-setting requests in the replicated store.** A `trust-setting` request is a record in the `trustJourney` store, reusing `AuthorizationRequestStore`'s hashing, dedup and rendered card. It is not written to `authorization-requests.json`, is exempt from `REQUEST_TTL_MS`, `MAX_GRANT_DURATION_MS`, the sweep and the shared pending cap, and executes a grant through `AdaptiveTrust`, not a mandate.
13. **One authority owner.** The current serving-lease holder (`LeaseCoordinator`/`FencedLease`) serializes grant mutations, receipt and approval consumption, offer resolution and narrowings, and admits every effect of a *governed* kind: one with a confirmed grant, a person-set grant or restriction, or no grant at all (new or uncovered). Other machines reach it through the existing `forwardToHolder` idiom; a 404 from an older holder counts as `LEASE_HOLDER_UNREACHABLE`. Receiving machines run route-level checks (revival mode, `routes.ts:36049-36075`) first and may refuse or prepare an ask locally; they never admit a governed effect from a cached grant. An effect covered only by an unchanged legacy grant is admitted on the machine where the session runs, as today, while that machine has applied the holder's `trustJourney` stream up to the head sequence the holder advertised, in an advert received within `trustJourney.legacyFreshnessMs` (default 60000, config); the `JournalSyncApplier` status label is never the test, because it reads `current` for a stream never heard from (`JournalSyncApplier.ts:1019`). Otherwise the admission is forwarded to the holder when it is reachable; when it is not, the effect continues locally as today (the partition window, Risks). Because a narrowing is pushed to every reachable machine before it is acknowledged (Multi-machine posture), a reachable machine never admits a legacy effect the person has already been told is narrowed. Single machine: everything runs locally.
14. **Repeated tasks are recognized by meaning, above the fixed floor (stage 3).** Justin: "How does an agent identify a repeated ask or repeated task? They are various levels of categories and we need to be very intelligent about this" [source: telegram 142440, 2026-11-15 12:29 PST], and "If approvals are way too granular than that will simply overwhelm and exhaust the user. However, if they were too general than a single approval might cover too much" [142440, 12:35 PST]. Rule 10 already requires this: what a person meant is decided by a model reading the context, never by a label. Two layers: the effect-kinds table stays the authority floor (service, operation, account, audience); a meaning layer, a ladder of task categories built from the person's own approvals, corrections and undos, decides what counts as the same yes and how wide an offer is. The meaning layer can only narrow what the floor allows, never widen it. Offers are sized to the record: the fewest asks that never cover more than the record shows the person is comfortable with (section 5a). Stages 0 to 2 keep the fixed key, and stage 0 does not wait on this.

## Proposed design

### 1. User outcome

A person tells their agent once how careful to be. After that the agent acts freely inside what was granted, every ask shows how the agent has done on that kind, one reply stops the asking when the record supports it, and a mistake narrows only the kind it happened in.

Measured on Echo through the existing maturation measurement surface: a baseline before stage 1 (rule 39), then asks per week, share approved unchanged, offers accepted or declined, corrections after a grant, and from stage 3 per category grant the asks approved unchanged under it (too narrow) and fit-admitted actions the person said should have been asked (too broad), each with method version, sample size and stated gaps. The surface shows this as history against the baseline, regressions included, and the weekly maturation review records any corrective action.

### 2. Settings and the always-ask floor

| Person's words | Existing level | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Ask me first | `approve-always` (`approve-first` displays the same) | Waits for a yes |
| Do it and show me | `log` | Acts and keeps each action in the record the person can open, with undo where one exists. Nothing is pushed per action; a report reaches the person only when it carries a usable result or something needing them, inside the existing notification budget |
| Just do it | `autonomous` | Acts; kept in the record |
| Don't do this | `blocked` | Refused, naming the setting |

**The ask.** When admission answers "ask" for a governed kind (decision 13), the hook refuses the call (exit 2), naming the kind and the grant that would allow it, and the server sends the ask into the person's direct conversation (kind, scope, the exact action, the section 4 line once stage 2 ships); where the work board is on, the same ask also appears as a card. The ask binds a stable action identity and the canonical effect payload (tool name and canonical input, including targets, content, scope and resource bounds).
- **The yes** is the person's platform reply to that ask message under decision 5's ingress rule, in the form the ask shows: `approve <code>` for one action, and for a grouped ask `approve <code> all` or `approve <code>` followed by item numbers. It is a displayed command with an exact binding, not a reading of words; any other reply approves nothing, is answered once with that instruction, and is stored verbatim with its message reference for stage 3 to read. From stage 3, a natural-language reply may also approve, read under section 5a's rules (isolated call, same route, given only the ask message exactly as it was sent to the person, with its item numbers and tuple labels, and the person's reply; never payload bodies or later descriptions; only what it unambiguously affirms; unclear approves nothing); with no model, only the command approves. This applies from a person whose acceptance for that adapter is current at consumption. The code binds the reply to that ask; it grants nothing standing and carries decision 5's disclosure. Without a current acceptance, the server first sends the acceptance message and holds the ask open. An action touching a floor item needs the full decision-5 approval (code and acceptance). PIN and phone taps on the card are recorded as signals and admit nothing.
- **One use.** The hook submits the action identity and payload on retry; the holder admits only the exact approved action under current restrictions and consumes the approval for one dispatch in the same durable event that records the admission. A changed payload, or a second call, asks again; repeating an admission request returns the recorded state, never a new permission; an uncertain dispatch is reconciled, never replayed. The consumed approval counts once in "asked / approved unchanged" and creates no grant.
- **Delivery.** If the direct-conversation send fails (the person has not opened a chat with the bot, `TelegramAdapter.ts:2569-2576`, or no owner id is set), the server posts one notice per kind in the conversation where the work is running, saying an answer is needed in the direct chat and how to open it; no reply there counts as a yes, and the hook's refusal names the same reason.
- **Expiry and resume.** An ask, and a yes not yet consumed, expire after `trustJourney.cardTtlDays`; a call after expiry asks again. When a yes is recorded, the server injects a resume notice naming the action identity into the originating session, or into that conversation's next session if it has ended.
- **Legacy kinds** with no person-set record keep today's advisory behaviour, admitted under current restrictions; an advisory answer never overrides an enforced ask.

**Always ask** is a check that runs before the level, and no setting, bundle or offer can lower it. It is the Purpose's five sign-off conditions:

1. money or a resource above the level the person named;
2. cannot be undone and falls outside the role;
3. speaks publicly in the person's name;
4. widens the agent's own role or authority;
5. touches a matter the person marked sensitive.

An action whose amount is unknown counts as floor item 1, and one whose sensitivity cannot be matched against the person's marks counts as item 5. Items 1 and 5 have dials the person owns; changing either toward less asking is a raise. No standing permission is ever offered for items 2, 3 or 4: they are the Purpose's own sign-off list, which no grant may lower (making public speech delegable would be a constitution change, out of scope). A kind at "Ask me first" is not yet a standing grant: the ask is the Purpose's "refusal says which grant would allow it", with the grant pre-filled.

### 3. Onboarding comfort question

One question in the onboarding conversation: "How careful should I be to start?" Each answer proposes per-kind grants from a static bundle table, for the services connected at that moment. The person can change any line in the same conversation.

| Answer | write | modify | delete |
|---|---|---|---|
| Careful | ask | ask | ask |
| Balanced | show me | ask | ask |
| Trusting | just do it | show me | ask |

- The answer is a proposal. Everything above "Ask me first" takes effect on one approval through decision 5, covering the whole bundle as one exact request. Without a recorded acceptance, onboarding asks for it first (one message back), and records nothing above "Ask me first" until it exists. An install with no direct-conversation adapter says so in "What I may do alone" and keeps its imported settings.
- `autonomous` is reachable when the person says so in their own words, through the same approval.
- A service connected later starts at "Ask me first" unless the recorded grant names it.
- Bundles default `onMistake` to `back-to-ask`, shown in the question.

### 4. The visible track record

A new append-only outcome record, owned by `AdaptiveTrust` and replicated, replaces the counter-only `TrustHistory`. Each row carries kind, grant id, person, machine, time, action identity, outcome, and the outcome's source type and evidence reference. The source type is set by the server, never by the writer.

| Outcome | Source types that produce it |
|---|---|
| done and checked | a person's message whose receipt meets the decision-5 ingress rule (any conversation where the platform authenticates the sender, topics included; no code or acceptance, since outcome rows grant nothing), sent as a platform reply to the server-sent message carrying that action's identity; or an outcome check shipped in Instar code for that effect (section 6a) |
| corrected (authoritative) | a person's message meeting the same ingress rule, bound to the action by a platform reply to its server-sent message, or, without that link, by a full-context model reading (labelled "read from your message"; can only narrow, never produces "checked"); or the undo route invoked by the person |
| undone | the undo route invoked by the person, or a rollback the person asked for |
| signal only | the correction sentinel, a board `reopen` or `confirm-done` (PIN or phone), and a phone confirmation, each shown with its source ("confirmed on a phone paired with your agent", "confirmed on console", "detected correction") |
| not yet checked | everything else, including agent certification, `agent-may-certify` items, an `owner-confirms` declaration with no confirmation, accepted evidence text, agent or LLM grades, and checks the agent added at runtime |

- A linked reply is read by a server-run, full-context model call, never by the session whose action it concerns. It yields "checked" only on an explicit confirmation of that action, "corrected" on a correction, and "not yet checked" for anything else or when no model is available. The reading and its input are logged.
- Signal rows count against the agent in what the person sees, which is the conservative direction, and support a narrowing proposal; they never count as checked and never change a setting on their own.
- Approval decisions stay in `ApprovalLedger` and the board's tap records, and are never counted as outcomes.
- Rows are deduplicated by action identity. A later correction or undo supersedes the displayed outcome without erasing history. An agent-reported row never corrects or supersedes a person's row, and the server sets every `decidedAt`.
- Counts and offer snapshots are computed by the server, never supplied by the proposing agent.

**Shown in two places, from one read:**

- **On every ask:** "Since 3 Nov: asked 12, approved unchanged 11, changed 1. Checked fine 9, corrected 1, not yet checked 2." The period runs from the kind's last raise; a narrowing never restarts it. The line shows the start of the window it actually displays (the last 30 days by default); an offer cites the full period it relies on.
- **In one list:** "What I may do alone", under Agent and by asking in chat. For each kind: the setting, who set it and when, its proof label with the disclosure, conditions, legacy label if any, and the same line.

**Gaps are stated:** "No record before 14 Nov", "Mac mini not counted since Tuesday". The rubber-stamp signal is shown as "approved quickly", is never evidence of quality, and can be dismissed only by the person.

**Cost.** The line is served from an in-memory index per (person, kind), built at boot and updated on each local write and replicated apply, as `WorkBoardService` does; no request reads a log file. Outcomes have their own replicated store with bounded retention; on each rotation a per-(person, kind) checkpoint record carries forward the counts and the corrections (from stage 3, for every correction, undo, changed approval and decline, its description, placement and floor scope), and the index rebuilds from the checkpoint plus the live tail.

### 5. Offers

- **Payload.** Installation, verified principal, the scope tuples, the category name, examples and exclusions (section 5a), conditions, source-authorization references, expected grant revision, from and to settings, and `recordSnapshotSha`. The server computes the snapshot and renders the card from the payload; the approved canonical payload covers every field.
- **Approval path.** Only decision 5 resolves it. The PIN route and the phone route refuse `trust-setting` as authority (403 naming the chat path); a phone signature may be attached as evidence.
- **Scope.** The exact (kind, account, audience, conditions) tuples the person approved under one task category, narrowed by that category, at any less restrictive level the record supports (decision 6); section 5a sizes it. Offers ship in stage 3 and every offer is for a category. Anything beyond the record, including a wider audience, a new account, condition removal, or a category broader than the record supports, is a bigger step.
- **One transaction.** The lease holder rechecks the expected revision and current restrictions, consumes the receipt and writes the grant as one durable event whose record key is `sha256(installation, requestId)`, so a retry or double execution folds to one grant. A moved revision returns 409 and supersedes the offer. Before committing, the holder sends a threaded reply saying the change is being recorded, with no unthreaded fallback; if the platform rejects it, the approval is refused. The holder then commits. If a person's raise or lower is still not durable after 6a's bounded repair, the holder sends a threaded follow-up saying it did not take effect and citing that attempt, and the request stays open; this is the one save failure that notifies, because the person was already told.
- **Silence is never consent.** An unanswered offer changes nothing. It has no time-based expiry: it stays open in "What I may do alone" until answered, withdrawn or superseded. It is superseded when the grant's revision changes or a correction, undo or narrowing lands for that kind after its snapshot; other new outcomes do not supersede it, and the grant records the snapshot the person saw. It may be shown again inline on the next ask of that kind and is never re-pushed on time alone.
- **From stage 3, "that kind" here means the offer's category.** A correction, undo or narrowing on any of its tuples supersedes it. A decline blocks the declined category, its ancestors and its descendants, and any category the placement question judges the same after a reorganization; other categories in the kind are unaffected. Earn-back stays keyed by the restored grant.
- **After a decline.** A declined or withdrawn offer stores its snapshot. A new offer for that kind needs one of: the person invites it; the person's stated decline condition is met; or at least one checked outcome recorded since the snapshot and the agent's judgment that the record or the person's needs materially changed. The new offer names what changed. Time alone never re-opens it; fallback: no offer. This replaces both cooldowns in G8.
- **No count cap.** An identical proposal is suppressed as a duplicate; overlapping offers cannot both be approved (the second gets 409). Which offers to present, and when, is the agent's judgment inside the notification budget.
- **One reply to decline** (`decline <code>`, or ignoring it). A reason is optional; the decline path and `recordApproval` change to accept a decline without one.
- **Bigger steps.** The agent may ask for a wider scope as an ordinary sign-off request with the record attached, under the same duplicate and revision rules. It is never a derived one-reply offer.

### 5a. Recognizing a repeated task (stage 3)

The person's goal is that the agent matches their trust without wearing them out: asks too fine-grained exhaust them, and one yes too broad covers what they never meant. A fixed `service.operation` label gets both wrong. It treats a reply to a teammate and a pitch to an investor as the same thing (both `email.write`), and it splits one real task, arranging a meeting, across `calendar.write` and `email.write`. Rule 10 already requires that what a person meant is decided by a model reading the context, never by a label; this section applies it. The label stays as the floor.

**The floor (deterministic, unchanged).** The effect-kinds table says what an action physically does: its kind, account and audience, and the sign-off items. Admission checks it first, exactly as in stages 0 to 2. Nothing in this section can admit an action the floor would not, and nothing here changes a sign-off item.

**Who judges, and where data goes.** Every meaning call in this section (description, placement, category identity, breadth and edge checks, fit, resizing) is a server-run call at the lease holder through the installation's own `IntelligenceProvider`, never the acting session. Every record carries its origin route: the framework, and the pool account the producing session ran on, stored on the ask record from stage 2. All accounts in the installation's pool for one framework count as one route, since sessions already move among them. A meaning call contains content from one route only and runs on that route, without cross-route fallback; before sending, the holder checks every included source (compared records, category text, examples, exclusions, labels, facts) is from that route, and never drops required context to make a call eligible. A category, its ladder comparisons and its grant belong to one route; records from another route are placed in that route's own ladder. An action from a session on another route asks. Extending a grant to another route is a bigger step whose card says "this grant's text and examples will also be read by <provider>". Stage 3 extends `requiredRoute` (today `codex-cli` only, `IntelligenceRouter.ts:1362-1363`) to every framework. A holder that lacks a route's credential returns "unsure" for it, reports one degradation naming the route, and "What I may do alone" lists the grants that cannot be checked from this holder; a record with no stored route is never sent to a model and stays at its floor scope. **Isolation.** Every meaning and reply-reading call is an isolated judgment call with no tools, MCP servers, connectors, filesystem access through tools, ambient agent instructions or secrets: Claude reviewer hardening with `noTools: true`, Codex `noLocalTools: true`, and equivalent isolation in each supported adapter (R30). If isolation cannot be established, the call is not launched: fit asks, a reading approves nothing, background work stays pending. Tiers: `fast` for descriptions; `capable` for placement, category identity, breadth and edge checks and resizing; `balanced` for fit, raised to `capable` where the shadow run shows it fits inside `fitJudgeTimeoutMs`; each choice is labelled unmeasured until the shadow counts back it. The acting session's wording reaches descriptions only, labelled as the agent's claim. No outside service is used, and "What I may do alone" names each route that receives this content. Every call is metered and recorded through the existing provenance and quality owners (R41, R58, R75).

**The meaning layer.**

- **What each record says.** Every ask, approval (unchanged or changed), decline, correction and undo gets a short plain-words description of what it was about ("a reply to Sarah about meeting times"), written asynchronously from the canonical effect payload and the ask's context line (the one-sentence reason rendered on the ask card). People are named from the team record and the relationships registry; a relation from the registry, which the agent can write, is shown as "listed as a teammate" and never decides placement by itself, and a person in neither is "someone you have not named". Cards show each tuple's address or handle beside the name. A record waiting for its description stays at its floor scope, and no description ever delays an ask, admission or card.
- **The ladder.** Descriptions are placed in a per-person ladder of categories, general to specific, for example "work > scheduling > meetings with my team > replies to Sarah about times" (depth is presentation guidance, never a reason to merge task scopes). The placement call proposes a record's path and, for each proposed category, judges whether it is the same as each existing sibling under the same parent, asked in both orders as a closed same / not-same / unsure question; anything short of "same" both ways makes a new category. Candidates are all siblings under the parent, with no word-overlap or label filter, paged when there are many, within `trustJourney.placementMaxCalls` (default 64) calls per record. A record descends only where the model judges containment in both orders, and categories merge only where it judges the same scope in both orders. When the budget runs out, placement stays pending and the record makes no derived offer. Reorganizing the ladder keeps every source record and never changes a committed grant. Pair verdicts are rows in `trustJourneyOutcomes`. This reuses the Correction Meaning Grouping method (`JevCorrectionGrouping.ts`: both orders, cached pair verdicts, every member matching every other, no A~B~C chains) with a new question about task scope (its own question version), not its standing-preference question, its same-kind word-overlap candidate filter, its words-mode fallback, or `JevCascade.askReferee` (which clips input); meaning calls use their own prompt builder under the whole-payload rule. A category is a label on records. It grants nothing.
- **A task can span kinds.** A category's records may carry several floor scopes (calendar and email for arranging a meeting). An offer for that category lists each one.

**Offer sizing (the central rule).** An offer is the fewest asks that never cover more than the record shows the person is comfortable with.

- **No category, no derived offer.** Every new offer to stop asking is for a category. If task scope cannot be established (no model, too few described records), no new offer is made; existing grants and one-action asks are unchanged, and an ask names the floor tuple as the grant that would allow it ("a grant for email to Sarah from your work account; I can offer one once your record shows what kind of task this is"). Earn-back is the exception: it offers to restore a grant the person previously approved, at exactly its approved scope and level.
- **Start narrow.** The offer is for the narrowest category that covers the repeats the person approved unchanged.
- **Climb only on spread.** A broader category is offered only when the person has approved distinct things under at least two of its child categories, and no unresolved correction sits under it. The two-child floor is definitional: with one child, the parent has no record beyond that child. A correction is unresolved when no later checked outcome on the same floor scope follows it and no stated exclusion covers it. How much spread is enough above the floor is the agent's judgment, with default guidance in the operator policy register (decision 11's pattern: guidance, never a gate). A person's own "wider" is not bound by this floor; it is a bigger step (below).
- **Breadth is tested.** Before a derived offer, the holder runs the fit check against the proposed category for every described record on the offer's tuples placed outside it, under the edge checks' lane, memo and bound. An outside record that returns "fits" or "unsure" shows the text covers more than its record: one the person did not approve unchanged becomes a stated exclusion; an approved-unchanged one means the offer is made for a narrower child that passes, or not at all.
- **Corrections and edits draw the edges.** Before an offer is made, every correction, undo, changed approval and decline whose floor scope overlaps the offer's is run through the fit check against the proposed category; any that fits or is unsure becomes a stated exclusion. Each is read in its authenticated context: an edit records the actual restriction it made, a decline is read as a deferral, a restriction or still unclear, and none becomes a permanent task exclusion from its label alone. These checks run in the background lane under the component budget, memoized by a fingerprint of the complete evaluated input (source row and its context, proposed category text, examples and exclusions, computed facts, model and question version); a category id never stands for its text, and the offer is assembled from the same snapshot that was checked. When the overlapping rows exceed `trustJourney.maxEdgeChecks` (default 50), the offer is made for a narrower child category whose rows fit the bound, or not at all; rows are never sampled or dropped. A row whose payload is no longer held is judged only on the part of its description written from the payload (descriptions keep that part separately), never on the context-line claim; for a correction, undo, decline or changed approval, anything but "does not fit" in both orders is a stated exclusion, and one with no payload-written part is stated as an exclusion of its whole floor scope. An approved-unchanged record without a payload cannot be one the offer rests on. A correction or undo in that floor scope with no description (a checkpointed or undescribable row) blocks a category offer for that floor scope until it is described, or is stated as an exclusion of that whole floor scope. An exclusion that depends on history ("first messages to people you have not written to") is passed to the fit check as a fact computed deterministically from the record, never left for the model to guess.
- **Floor scope from the record.** The offer's floor scope is the set of exact (kind, account, audience, conditions) tuples the person approved under the category, each carrying its source-authorization reference. No combination of fields from different tuples is covered. A derived offer adds no recipient. A bigger step, never a derived offer, may propose a tuple whose audience is a rule the effect-kinds table evaluates deterministically at act time: a member of the owner's signed team record, or the sender of the message being answered, only where the server itself fetches the referenced message through the account in the action's tuple, confirms that same account received it, and confirms that its sender, and any reply-to address, is a single address that is the action's only recipient; a message carrying a list or group marker the service exposes (for email `List-Id`, or a known group address) asks. A reference read from the tool input alone is a claim and yields unknown scope, which asks; a service with no such lookup is not offered this rule. Team-record rules count the owner and members, never guests. The card names the rule, who it covers today, and that it will cover people who match it later; a reply-to-sender card says "this covers a reply to anyone who writes to you, including people you have never written to". The relationships registry never defines such a rule.
- **The card.** The server writes every word of it from the ladder and the record: the category name from the ladder record by id; two or three examples it chooses from approved-unchanged records under the category (the most recent, one per distinct tuple); every exclusion; the tuples; the section 4 line for the category; and "this covers future actions of this kind until you change it". The acting session supplies only the category id. When the person replies "narrower" or "wider" in their own words, a server-run call maps those words to a ladder category or proposes a new one, and the server prepares a new card with a new code. A narrower card is a derived offer. A wider one, and any card whose tuples or category go beyond the record, is a bigger step (section 5): it says plainly what it adds ("this would also cover meetings with Dan and Priya, which you have not approved yet") and is approved through decision 5. A narrowing the person states applies at once (section 6, Lowering).

**The grant.** A category grant records the category's name, examples and exclusions as text and its tuples, all inside the proposal hash, written with `minReader: 3` (section 6a). Later ladder changes (a rename, a merge) never change a committed grant: the grant keeps the text the person approved. Only two things change it. A narrowing the person makes (a new exclusion, a dropped tuple) is appended as a narrowing record. A widening (a new example, a tuple, a broader category) is a new revision approved through decision 5. The load-time proof check verifies the approved hash plus the chain of narrowing records.

**At act time.** For a category grant, admission is: floor first (the action's tuple matches a grant tuple exactly, or matches an audience-rule tuple as that rule evaluates now, and no sign-off item applies), then fit. Fit is judged at the holder from the canonical payload, the grant's category, examples and exclusions, the server's label for the matched tuple as the card showed it, and any computed history facts only. The session's wording and the context line never reach the fit judge (R27: a judge is not handed the expected answer); they feed descriptions only, labelled as the agent's claim. The payload sits in a delimited field and is data, never instructions; text in it that addresses the judge or asserts a verdict yields "unsure". The judge reads the whole payload: one over the judge's input bound, or with content it cannot read (attachments, binary), asks and says so, and fit is never judged on a fragment. The judge returns exactly one of fits / does not fit / unsure and names the example or feature it matched. Both option orders are two separate provider calls run concurrently, both inside the fit budget; a late or failed call counts as unsure, and only "fits" in both admits. The verdict is bound to (grant revision, narrowing-chain head, pending-marks hash, payload hash, hash of the computed history facts, model, question version); the holder holds no lock while waiting, then recomputes the history facts and rechecks current restrictions, the grant revision, the narrowing-chain head and its own fencing token before committing the admission. Any change, including a new narrowing, voids the verdict and re-judges; a cached verdict never carries dispatch authority by itself. Anything short of "fits" asks, and the ask says why ("I wasn't sure this is one of 'replies to Sarah about times'").

- **The person's answer.** A yes (read as section 2 says) admits that one action and is kept as evidence for the category. If a server-run reading finds the reply means it should count from now on, the server prepares a card adding it as an example, approved through decision 5. A no refuses that action. Only a stated standing restriction becomes a narrowing: the server adds the exclusion at once, quoting the person's words with the message reference, and replies with its text. A one-action refusal, deferral or content change creates no standing exclusion and shapes the next offer's edges. If it is unclear, the refusal acknowledgement asks once in the same thread, "Just this one, or always ask for ones like it?"; until answered, the grant carries a pending mark: a `trustJourney` record holding the server-written description of the declined action and the message reference. The fit judge receives every pending mark as a temporary exclusion, and the hash of the grant's pending marks joins the verdict binding and the commit recheck, so a mark voids earlier verdicts. Either answer clears it; it never expires on time and is listed in "What I may do alone". It is not a narrowing record. With no model, the action is refused and the grant is unchanged.
- **Bursts.** Verdicts are memoized by the full binding above, so a retry with identical inputs costs nothing. Each action is judged in its own calls; no call carries two actions' payloads. Admissions under one grant within `trustJourney.fitBatchWindowMs` (default 2000), or one bulk plan, run concurrently in the interactive lane; any not judged inside the budget ask, and the asks they produce coalesce into one ask message listing the actions under stable item numbers bound to their exact identities and payloads; the request hash covers every listed payload. No per-item message is sent unless the person asks for one or an unresolved selection needs it, and the notification budget covers every message. The platform reply identifies the request; the server's reading of it (section 2) admits only the listed actions the person unambiguously approved: an unqualified yes to the whole set approves all, "yes except Dan" approves all but Dan's, and anything unclear gets one clarification for those items and admits nothing unresolved. Each approved action is consumed once against its own identity and payload hash. An item whose chain head or history facts change after an earlier item commits is re-judged before admission. An action touching a floor item is never coalesced.
- **Time budget.** Nested and fixed in config: `trustJourney.fitJudgeTimeoutMs` (default 8000) covers queue wait and the call; admission forwards use `trustJourney.admissionForwardTimeoutMs` (default fit + 4000) instead of the generic forward timeout; the hook's in-script abort and the Codex handler timeout for this hook are set above that (forward + 3000). Stage 3 ships these through the always-overwritten hook script and the `installCodexHooks` migration; a test asserts hook > forward > fit. Expired work never starts a provider call and never later admits.
- **Budget.** The trust journey owns one `LlmQueue` at the holder with its own daily cap (`trustJourney.llm.maxDailyCents`, default 100). Fit runs in the interactive lane; descriptions, placement, identity checks and backfill run in the background lane under a component budget that cannot consume fit's reserve. Fit passes a cost estimate computed from the payload's token count, and day totals are restored from the metering rows at boot and on handover. When fit cannot run (cap spent, no provider), category grants ask; the person gets one notice naming the affected grants, and waiting actions are listed in "What I may do alone", not pushed one by one.

**Fit is earned before it admits.** Stage 3 first runs fit in shadow on the development agent. For each ask under a kind with a candidate category (the category offer sizing would propose at that moment, with its exclusions), fit is judged against that candidate and logged with the person's answer. A false admission is "fits" on an ask the person declined or changed; an unnecessary ask is anything short of "fits" on an ask the person approved unchanged. Both are upper bounds and labelled as such. The set includes seeded same-floor, different-task cases and payload-instruction cases. The named success criteria: no "fits" on any seeded different-task or payload-instruction case; every real false admission reviewed and attributed to scope or content; fewer asks than ask-first on approved-unchanged records. The maturation review is keyed by (question version, model) and records these counts, latency and a judgment over them; an installation whose model has no recorded review makes no category offer. Reviewed (question version, model) pairs are a code constant, `src/data/fit-reviews.ts`, changed only by a reviewed release; an installation reads nothing else. A new question version re-enters shadow. Missing evidence records `incomplete`.

**On every installation.** Where `trustJourney.enabled` and `meaningLayer.enabled` are both on, fit also runs in shadow in the background lane on each ask that has a candidate category. A category offer needs both of these on that installation's current fit model and question version: "fits" in both orders for every approved record the offer rests on, and short of "fits" for every decline, correction, undo and changed approval in its floor scope that the offer does not state as an exclusion. The fit model is the model id recorded on the grant when its check last passed, and fit calls pin it (`strictModelPin`). The grant records one fit model per route. If that model cannot serve, including after a handover, the verdict is "unsure" and one re-check per (grant, model) on the same route is queued in the background lane; it passes only for a model with a recorded review for the current question version, and otherwise the grant asks until one exists. It runs on the grant's card examples, whose canonical payloads are kept in `trustJourney` with the grant and deleted with it; other source records are re-checked only while their payload is held, and a rotated payload is not a failure. A grant that fails the re-check asks, with one notice.

**What a fit verdict proves (P20).** It is a fallible judgment that the person would consider this action covered, not proof of their intent. It is corroborated only by what the person does with actions they see: corrections and undos, and checked outcomes. Silence is unknown, never confirmation. When it cannot be measured, the answer is ask.

**Measured.** Per category grant, shown with the section 4 line: asks approved unchanged under it (too narrow), and fit-admitted actions the person later said should have been asked (too broad). A correction of content alone is a mistake under `onMistake`, not a fit error. Fit errors are graded and narrow the grant under `onMistake`.

**Storage.** Approved grant text, tuples, proofs, narrowing records, ladder categories and placements are records in `trustJourney`. Descriptions and fit verdicts (with the payload hash and description, never the payload) are rows in `trustJourneyOutcomes` under its bounded retention, and each rotation's checkpoint carries per-grant fit counts and fit errors forward. Pair verdicts are kept only for the current sibling sets and can be rebuilt. A (person, category) projection over the same rows serves the category's record line, rebuilt from placements, as the (person, kind) index is.

**Records from before stage 3.** Stage 2 rows reference the canonical effect payload held in the ask record and keep the context line (stage 2 frontload). At stage 3, records whose payload is still held are described at the holder in the background lane, newest first, at most `trustJourney.backfillPerDay` (default 200), resumable from a cursor record, never at boot, labelled "described later". Records without a payload stay at their floor scope, uncategorized; their corrections fall under the rule above.

### 6. Raising and lowering

`OperatorProof` is a type only the lease holder's approval check constructs, from a consumed decision-5 receipt matched to the request's proposal hash under a current acceptance. The receipt, acceptance reference and proposal hash are stored on the grant record.

**Raising.** `AdaptiveTrust.grantTrust`, the bundle write, `/autonomy/profile`, `PATCH /config` and `TrustRecovery.acceptRecovery` refuse a raise (decision 4) without an `OperatorProof` whose proposal hash equals the exact change.

**Checked at load.** Whenever a confirmed grant is loaded or applied, the holder checks its exact proposal hash and durable receipt-consumption record, including that the referenced acceptance was in force when the consumption committed. New consumption requires an acceptance still current at commit; withdrawal prevents later consumption and never invalidates grants already committed. Results are cached per grant revision; current restrictions are still checked at admission. A failed check supplies no permission and never removes a restriction: the grant is rebuilt once from the committed streams, and only if it still fails is the person told, in one notice per failed rebuild listing the affected kinds, citing the attempt. This catches inconsistent projections, hand edits and hallucinated writes; it does not stop a forger who controls the host (Threat model).

**Lowering.** A lower applies at once from the person's message (through any adapter, the person's own words), a recorded condition the person set (section 7), or an existing emergency stop. The receiving machine applies it locally and forwards it to the holder, which commits it. After an incident without such a condition, the agent explains and proposes the narrowing as a request; an agent-originated lower is a proposal, not applied.

**The signal routes.** `POST /approvals` and `POST /autonomy/elevation/record` accept rows that feed the record only from the decision-5 path, with `approver` set to the principal; agent-reported rows are kept, labelled agent-reported, and never count toward an offer. `POST /autonomy/elevation/dismiss-rubber-stamp` needs the person.

**In chat.** The person says yes; the agent prepares the request; the server sends the card; the person replies `approve <code>` to it.

### 6a. Enforcement, persistence and recovery

- **Effect kinds.** Stage 0 adds a server-owned table, `src/data/effect-kinds.ts`, covering exactly the effects that reach `ExternalOperationGate.evaluate` today (the generated external-operation hook's matchers, its only caller, together with the route's revival-mode block) plus work-board dispatch, each mapped to `{service, operation, account rule, audience rule, floor rules, outcome check}`. The hook migration sends `tool_name` and `tool_input` to `/operations/evaluate`; the server, not the hook, derives the kind, the account (the connected service's account), the audience (from the entry's named input fields, or `self` where the effect has no recipient) and the floor items from them. An entry with no audience rule yields unknown scope, which asks. Routes not gated today are not kinds and are unchanged; a later table entry ships with a migration that records a legacy grant at that effect's current behaviour. The server derives the kind from the matched entry; a caller-supplied classification can only narrow; no match asks. The hook is agent-editable, so for hook-mediated effects this is a checkpoint for an honest agent (Threat model).
- **Admission.** Admission follows decision 13. For a confirmed grant, the person's explicit restrictions and the five sign-off conditions are the authority constraints; the gate's risk classification and bulk planning (and any LLM assessment, which the live gate does not wire today) inform how the work is carried out (checkpoints, plans shown in the record) but do not require another approval for covered work, and admission never waits on an LLM, with one exception: from stage 3, an action under a category grant waits for the bounded fit check (section 5a), and anything short of "fits" in both orders asks. The hook migration ships with stage 0. For every governed kind, an enforced ask, a malformed admission response, a timeout or an unreachable holder refuses the call (exit 2) and reports the pending ask or unavailable admission so the session can continue other work. The server keeps a derived file, `.instar/state/trust-legacy-kinds.json`, rebuilt from the grant records at boot and on every grant change, listing exact tool names: the read entries for every service with a governed kind, and the tool names of unchanged legacy kinds as the effect-kinds table maps them on this machine. When the server is unreachable or times out, the hook checks this file before its name-based read check: a call passes only if its exact name is listed as a read or as a legacy kind, and every other call is refused (exit 2). A missing or unreadable file refuses every non-read call when `trustJourney.legacyImportedAt` is present in `config.json`; with no file and no marker the hook keeps today's fallback. The server writes the file, possibly empty, when the import commits. Listed legacy tool names keep today's exit-0 fallback. Both timeouts rise above the forward timeout: the hook's in-script abort (`PostUpdateMigrator.ts:24382`) and the Codex handler timeout for this hook in `installCodexHooks` (`:65-68`), since Codex kills a slow hook and proceeds; the script is always overwritten on update, and the Claude settings entry already allows enough time. Reading keeps its fast path.
- **Legacy kinds and the table.** A kind with an unchanged legacy grant keeps today's behaviour whether or not the table matches it; "no match asks" and unknown-scope asks apply only to governed kinds.
- **The record.** `ApprovalLedger.summarizeRows` counts raw rows with no person or time filter and no correction supersession, so the record reads authenticated, deduplicated decisions through the index (section 4).
- **Durable change log.** Stage 0 makes the trust change log a replicated, append-only record stream; each row names its proof. The holder commits a change before it takes effect.
- **Corrupt or missing state.** The server starts, and communication and emergency stop continue. Grants and restrictions are rebuilt from the committed streams, using peers and the backup journal where available. A recovered grant is usable only when its restriction history is established; an older backup or projection alone does not establish that no later narrowing occurred. Every known restriction is preserved, including `blocked`. A kind with unresolved restriction history is unavailable for effects, never merely "Ask me first". After bounded recovery fails, the person receives an exact recovery request through decision 5 naming the affected scope, the recovered restrictions, the evidence gap and a proposed replacement setting; the replacement is committed before any effect is admitted, keeps the loss disclosure, and later-arriving restrictions reconcile under the conflict rule. Recovering settings never replays an uncertain effect. This works on one machine.
- **Self-repair.** Storage faults go through bounded, audited repair (reload, rebuild from the streams, restore and reconcile), at most three attempts per fault, then escalate; a fault of the same class that returns after a repair reported success escalates on its second return. A failed save is a degradation report plus retry, never a direct notice. The person is told only when repair fails or a restriction cannot be recovered.
- **Backup, config, rollback.** Both `trustJourney` stores' journal files join the backup, including every origin's files held on this machine (own and peer replicas), not only its own. `trustJourney` config is added by `migrateConfig` with existence checks. Authority replication and enforcement are part of stage 0 and forced on regardless of the feature flag. `trustJourney.enabled: false` disables onboarding, offers, the journey's displays and `per-kind` board execution; it never disables grant and restriction enforcement, the floor check, proof checks, replay protection or authority replication. A binary downgrade that cannot enforce recorded restrictions cannot execute affected operations (section 9's coherence flag). Stage 0 also ships the reader rule stage 3 needs: a grant record whose `minReader` is above the build's own, or that carries fields the build does not know, is treated as absent ("Ask me first") for admission, never as its floor kind, so a category grant never reads as a whole-kind grant on an older holder or after a rollback. `trustJourney.meaningLayer.enabled: false` makes category grants ask and stops category offers; it never admits by floor scope alone.

### 7. Narrowing after a mistake

- **Per grant.** The grant that admitted the action narrows; other grants and kinds are untouched.
- **Person-set condition.** Each grant carries `onMistake`: `back-to-ask` or `keep`.
- **What changes a setting, deterministically.** An authoritative `corrected` or `undone` row (section 4) on a grant with `back-to-ask` applies the drop, when the row's source is the grant's person or a person whose things the action touched. Signal rows (sentinel detections, PIN or phone board taps) show on the record, supersede an open offer and may support a narrowing proposal, but change no setting. The agent's judgment over events with no row can only propose a narrowing. If the agent thinks a drop was harmless it says so and offers earn-back. A provider outage or harmless retry produces no row.
- **What the agent does.** It explains, repairs what it can, and reports once.
- **Earn-back.** When the checked outcomes since the incident support it (decision 11's floor; guidance 10), `TrustRecovery` raises an offer through section 5, and `acceptRecovery`, now reached through the offer, restores the level with proof.

### 8. Team rules

- Grants are keyed by person. The owner's grants are the ceiling for the role.
- The effective level is the most restrictive of the owner's ceiling and the restriction of every person whose things the action touches.
- A member may lower settings for their own things. A raise above the owner's ceiling is refused and names the owner's grant.
- A record earned with one person never counts toward another person's offer.
- Guests have no settings.
- Member settings do not act on the board until the board's member phase.

### 9. The work board under option C

Checked against `WorkCardStore.ts`, `WorkBoardService.ts`, `workBoardViews.ts` and `workBoardRoutes.ts` on main (G11). This spec amends `work-board-asks.md`'s "always for outward cards" for the `per-kind` policy only.

- **Fields.** `kind`, `scope` (`{account, audience}`, a list when the card spans operations) and `floor` (sign-off item ids) join `MATERIAL_FIELDS`, the creation and edit allowlists, the schema, the material hash and the views; changing any of them voids an approval. Missing or unknown scope requires approval. An agent-supplied kind, scope or floor is a claim: at dispatch the effect-kinds table classifies the real action, and an action wider than the card claimed asks and is recorded against the claimed kind.
- **Policy.** `per-kind` is a third value of the replicated `policy` record, added to `POLICIES`, `WorkBoardService.policy()`, `policyAt` (`WorkCardStore.ts:476`, which today coerces anything but `outward-only` to `every-agent-card`) and the validators at `WorkBoardService.ts:571` and `workBoardRoutes.ts:256,265`. Any policy change other than to `every-agent-card` needs an `OperatorProof`; the PIN settings route accepts only `every-agent-card`. `By.proof` gains `'accepted-account'`.
- **Mixed builds.** An older build drops the new fields and rejects the new policy value, so no safe downgrade is assumed. `trustJourney.perKindPolicy` joins `COHERENCE_CRITICAL_FLAGS` (the mixed-build test matrix covers the manifest-hash change). `per-kind` may be written only while every enrolled member in `MachinePoolRegistry` carries it resolved true in its `coherenceAdvert` (a member with no advert counts as not knowing it), and the server writes `every-agent-card` immediately before `per-kind`, so an older build's newest readable policy is the strict one. A returning member that does not carry it cannot execute `per-kind` cards.
- **Admission under `per-kind`.** `foldCard` takes `opts.grantLevel(kind, scope)`, supplied by `WorkBoardService` from the grant view, and the dispatch record carries the grant revision that admitted the card (the fold stays pure). Withdrawn, held, archived and unreadable cards behave as today. A floor flag, a `blocked` or `approve-always` kind, or any uncovered scope requires approval. Otherwise approval is not required when the effective grant for every listed scope is `log` or `autonomous`. This applies to `outward` cards too, because the policy is itself a proven raise and dispatch is admitted by the holder.
- **Category grants on the board.** A category grant is never judged on card wording, which the agent writes. `foldCard` takes a second pure input, `opts.categoryTuple(kind, scope)`, true when the tuple is in a category grant and no decision-3 restriction (a `requireApproval` entry, a person-set or later ask, "Don't do this", a narrowing) applies to it. Under `per-kind`, a card whose every listed tuple is true dispatches without a card approval, overriding the `approve-always` line only for a bundle ask that grant replaced; each governed effect inside it is admitted under decision 13 at act time, with fit judged on the real payload (section 5a), and anything short of "fits" asks.
- **Changes propagate.** A grant or policy change refolds every card of the affected kinds and produces durable, deduplicated pickup or stop causes through the existing controller, so a card does not keep waiting after a grant or keep running after a withdrawal. Dispatch re-evaluates at the holder; a revision mismatch re-asks.
- **On the card.** An asking card shows the section 4 line and, when a category is eligible, says "Stop asking for these?" and points to the request card in the person's direct chat. The offer is never a card tap.
- **Record from the board.** Board `confirm-done` and `reopen` taps are signal rows (section 4). A finish on the agent's own evidence is not yet checked. There is no second write to `ApprovalLedger`. A card tap approves the card, never a protected effect or a trust widening: card approval only dispatches the card, and each governed effect inside it is still admitted under decision 13: by its grant, or by its own section 2 yes.
- `every-agent-card` stays selectable as a person's explicit choice. `outward-only` is not used for option C.

## Multi-machine posture

- **Grants, proofs, receipts, acceptances, the change log, offers, outcomes and checkpoints are `unified`.** Two store/kind pairs, each one kind per store as the registry requires: `trustJourney` → `trust-grant-record` (grants, proofs, receipts, acceptances, offers, change log; retained in full, with resolved receipts compacted to their key) and `trustJourneyOutcomes` → `trust-outcome-record` (bounded, with checkpoints). Each has its `JournalKind`, retention entry, schema, state-sync config, backup entry and apply and index wiring, and is emitted through `ReplicatedRecordEmitter` into `CoherenceJournal`. Both `multiMachine.stateSync` flags are forced on with stage 0, as `workBoard` is. An emitter no-op is a failed write, never a silent one.
- **The meaning layer is `unified`.** Ladder categories, placements, category grants and their narrowing records live in `trustJourney`; descriptions and fit verdicts are rows in `trustJourneyOutcomes` (section 5a, Storage), both written by the lease holder, so every machine sees the same categories and offers. Model calls run at the holder; a holder with no provider asks for category grants and makes no category offer, and reports a degradation naming itself. Cached pair verdicts carry their model and question version and are kept across a handover.
- **One owner.** The lease holder commits every mutation and consumption and admits every governed effect (decision 13). Unchanged legacy effects are admitted locally while the machine holds the holder's advertised head from a fresh advert (decision 13), and forwarded otherwise when the holder is reachable. Legacy grants are unified records that carry their machine id, because they reproduce that machine's own gate config. A narrowing applies at once on the machine that received it, makes its kind governed, and is pushed synchronously to the holder and every reachable machine before the person is told it is in force. A machine acknowledges the push only after the narrowing is committed to its local grant state, and local legacy admission reads that state under the same lock, so an admission evaluated after the apply sees it; a machine whose push failed is named "not yet updated". The acknowledgement names which machines have it ("Narrowed here and on the Mac mini; <machine> not yet updated"). Effects admitted before a narrowing are reported as such.
- **Holder unreachable or changing.** Governed effects wait (fail closed); unchanged legacy effects continue locally, as they do today; communication, the record display and emergency stop continue. A narrowing received while the holder is unreachable is in force on the receiving machine at once and is forwarded when the holder returns. A machine cut off from both the holder and the receiving machine cannot learn of a narrowing until it reconnects; that window is disclosed in the line above and covered by the existing emergency stop. A new holder establishes the committed head from every source stream before it admits anything.
- **Adapter receipts.** The machine that receives a platform message writes the receipt record; the holder consumes it. Which machine receives is the existing per-adapter polling assignment.
- **Conflicts.** Concurrent raise and lower resolve to the more restrictive level, unless the raise is an explicit restoration naming the lower it observed.
- **Outcome rows merge by union.** An unreachable machine shows as a stated gap, and an offer that depends on its rows waits until the gap is filled.
- **Single machine:** the holder is the only machine; everything works with no peer.
- **No machine-local state surface is introduced.** Record-line projections and the in-memory index are derived from unified records and rebuilt on boot. PIN attempt counters and the lifeline secret are existing operational state this spec does not change.

## Team posture

- **Team access is untouched.** Who is on the team and what each person can see stays in the owner's signed team record; this spec never grants or changes team access. Trust grants concern what the agent may do with a person's own things, and are approved by that person through decision 5.
- **Who sees:** each person sees their own settings and record. The owner sees the role-level grants they made; a member's own counts and settings are visible to the owner only where the member shared them through the signed team record.
- **Who acts:** each person on their own settings, and the owner on the ceiling.
- **The meaning layer is per person.** A person's descriptions, categories and fit verdicts are private to that person; the owner sees them only where the member shared them through the signed team record.
- **Default:** private to the person.
- **Sharing:** only through the owner's team record.
- **Team of one:** loses nothing.

- **Stage 4: onboarding and team.** The comfort question, the existing-owner migration, and member rules. *Proof:* a fresh install completes in one conversation with grants recorded through a chat approval; member and guest scenarios run in throwaway homes.

Every stage adds its capability to the agent's briefing through an awareness entry (`src/data/awareness/`).

## Risks

- **Disk-level forgery.** An agent that edits its own disk, code, key files or the lifeline secret can forge an approval. This is the custody gap the approval-custody work closes (Threat model); this spec's labels never claim otherwise.
- **The account is shared.** The disclosure says so on every approval; the person can withdraw the acceptance at any time, which stops new raises and leaves committed grants as they are.
- **No Face ID approval yet.** Phone signatures are evidence only until the custody work lands. Approving is one chat reply, which is phone-completable.
- **Installs with no direct-conversation adapter** (app-only) cannot raise trust; their imported settings keep working, and "What I may do alone" says why. Constitution wording item 9, or the custody work, closes this.
- **New services.** A service first connected after stage 0 asks on its first write; this is the one new restriction, shown in "What I may do alone".
- **Partitioned machine.** A machine cut off from the holder and from the machine that took a narrowing keeps today's legacy behaviour on unchanged legacy kinds until it reconnects; the acknowledgement names the machines that have the narrowing, so the person knows it is not yet everywhere, and emergency stop is unaffected. Routing every legacy effect through the holder would close this window but stop every existing agent's work whenever the holder is unreachable, which an update must not do; this trade-off is recorded in the convergence report.
- **Mandate floor grants.** PIN-approved Slack floor mandates predate this spec and are reported separately (Threat model).
- **Holder outage.** Governed effects wait until a holder serves; waiting is the safe direction, and the existing failover restores a holder.
- **Approval fatigue read as trust.** Offers need checked outcomes, not approvals.
- **Kind misclassification.** Derived from the effect-kinds table; unsure means ask; graded in retrospect.
- **A category too wide or too narrow.** Too wide covers what the person did not mean; too narrow asks again for what they did. Offers start at the narrowest covering category, climb only on spread, carry every correction as an exclusion, and say plainly what goes beyond the record; the person can resize before saying yes. Fit errors are graded and narrow under `onMistake`.
- **Wording an action to fit.** The fit check reads the real payload at the holder, never the agent's description, and the floor scope is deterministic, so a mis-worded or injected payload is bounded by the exact tuples the person granted for that task and by the sign-off items; inside them, the delimited payload, the unsure-on-address rule, the shadow run and graded fit errors are the protection, and an injected pitch to Sarah under a scheduling grant is the residual case they target.
- **Model unavailable.** Category grants ask and no category offer is made; other grants and legacy kinds are unaffected; the person gets one notice, not one per action.
- **Over-asking.** A model outage, a spent budget or a slow holder fails toward asking, which is the exhaustion the person named. Asks are coalesced and counted, the "too narrow" measure shows them, and the budget has its own reserve for fit.
- **Offer nagging.** Duplicate suppression, the notification budget and the decline openings.
- **Report flooding** from "show me". Actions stay in the record; only usable results or needs reach the person.
