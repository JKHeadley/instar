# A scheduled job that quietly stops now tells its operator

Luna lost roughly a week of scheduled client work after the serving lease moved from a laptop to a Mac Studio. Some jobs existed only on the laptop; others existed on the Studio but their gates needed a folder found only on the laptop. Those gates skipped repeatedly, and the scheduler recorded the skips without showing the operator a problem. The first repair is to make that silence visible on existing installations, without changing when or where a job runs.

An enabled job now raises an operator message after three gate or machine-scope skips. The scheduler also checks every five minutes for enabled jobs that have not completed for past its scheduled time plus a short grace period. A job pinned to another machine is visible immediately when the current scheduler holds the lease. A completed success closes the alert episode. The state survives a server restart, and reminders are limited to once a day while the problem remains. A message is counted as delivered only after the send succeeds. A failed send is logged and retried after at least one hour. A standby does not write alert state or speak for the agent.

This step does not turn on git-sync for Luna. Luna's repo contains token-bearing config backups and machine-local files; enabling the existing add-all sync would risk pushing them. The later job portability work must use an explicit allowlist and never infer consent to sync everything. The alert here reports missing coverage so Justin can see the problem while the lease and job replication changes are built.

To verify the change, tests create a job that skips three times, confirm one alert, then confirm that only a completed success clears it. Another test gives a job an old first-seen date and checks the overdue alert. A standby test confirms no alert state is written there. This change is reversible by reverting the scheduler code; it does not migrate or delete job definitions or history.
