When Instar decides to quietly record a routine notification instead of sending it, it also writes one line about that decision to a log file on disk. Until now, every one of those lines went through its own small two-step write, and the writes stood in a single line behind each other. Normally that's fine. But when a lot of routine notifications arrive at once, say a thousand, a thousand tiny writes pile up. Anything that waits for the log to be finished, like shutting the notification system down cleanly, has to wait for every one in turn. On a busy machine that took seven to eleven seconds, long enough to trip a test's ten-second limit and fail a verification run for a change that had nothing to do with it. Now the lines that are waiting together are written together, in one go and in the same order. Nothing about what gets recorded changes. The waiting just shrinks from thousands of steps to a handful.
