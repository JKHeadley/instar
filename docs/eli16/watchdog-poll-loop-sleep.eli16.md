Instar has a watchdog that looks for commands that seem stuck and interrupts them, so a session doesn't freeze forever. Some commands are meant to wait, though. A common one is a small loop that checks something, sleeps fifteen seconds, and checks again, printing nothing until the thing it's watching changes. The watchdog already knew a plain "sleep" isn't stuck. But it looked at the loop as a whole, saw a quiet command that had been running for minutes, and often decided it was stuck. Four of those watches were stopped on one machine this morning, in the middle of a live sign-in, and four of my own waits for a turn were stopped on another. Now, when the watchdog sees a timed sleep running inside that loop, or saw one there in the last five minutes, it leaves the loop alone. A loop that stops sleeping, because it's really stuck inside its check, loses that protection after five minutes. No loop is protected for more than two hours.
