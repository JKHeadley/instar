## What Changed

The Instar iPhone app can now show the agent's plans, what it learned about its owner, and the other agents it works with natively. Three new read-only routes answer a paired owner phone (or the agent's token): `GET /mobile/plans` (projects and initiatives grouped the way the console groups them: needs you, in flight, idle, older and still open, finished; each with its round or step line and what it waits for; read from this machine and every online machine, each plan naming the machine it lives on), `GET /mobile/learned` (the learned preferences from the session-start block with how sure the agent is and how often it saw them, and the scrubbed corrections it is still watching or already settled, from every machine that answers) and `GET /mobile/agents` (other agents by the name they claim, their pairing state and delivery health in the console's words, and this machine's channel registry verdicts: working, broken, not set up, can't tell). They are trimmed summaries: no record ids, paths, links, topic ids, agent fingerprints, invite material, message bodies, channel evidence text or raw errors, and all shown text is cleaned and scrubbed. Only owner phones may read them (an MSP phone gets 403 `owner-phone-required`); they share their own 20-per-minute allowance, and each answer is built at most once every 15 seconds. A machine that did not answer or is offline is named, and the headline says the answer covers the machines that answered. The raw `/initiatives`, `/projects`, `/preferences/session-context`, `/corrections`, `/channels` and `/threadline/*` routes stay closed to phones. A failed read records a `MobilePlansPeople.read` degradation.

The confirm page for saying yes to a recommended service now exists. It shows what the service involves, takes the dashboard PIN and a single-use token, and saves the yes along with the machine it authorizes. It records the approval and opens a tracked commitment for setup. No real service can reach it yet, because none has a proven setup, so nothing changes today.

## What to Tell Your User

Once the app update with these screens is on your phone, your agent's page in the app has a "Plans and people" section: Plans, What I've learned about you, and Other agents. Plans shows what needs your OK first, then what is moving and what has gone quiet. What I've learned shows the habits I picked up from your corrections and the ones I'm still watching. Other agents shows who I'm paired with, who has answered lately, and which ways of reaching you and other agents work right now. These screens only look: to change anything, tell me in chat or use the dashboard. Only the owner's phone sees them.

Nothing changes for you yet. This prepares the page you'll use to say yes to a service once I've proven I can set it up.

## Summary of New Capabilities

- The owner's phone can see every plan across machines, grouped by what needs them, what is moving, and what is finished.
- The owner's phone can see the preferences the agent learned and the corrections it is still watching.
- The owner's phone can see other agents' pairing and delivery health, and which channels work on this machine.

`GET` and `POST /recommended-services/:id/confirm`, and a page at `/dashboard/service-yes`. Both answer that a service isn't available until it has a proven setup.

## Evidence

Unit tests cover both sides of each rule: the 7-day and 14-day grouping lines, every needs-you cause, child plans folding into their project's round line, the same plan name on two machines shown as two rows, a future-dated time read as no time, an unknown status still listed, a newer correction copy replacing this machine's while a future-dated or tied one does not, a peer's switched-off answer told apart from a failure, offline and unanswering machines scoping the headline, the finished and overall caps; preference line parsing with and without the confidence suffix, correction watching versus earlier (noise never watched), a full page marking the list incomplete, an unknown status never reading as saved; every delivery-health kind, "no record yet" versus "can't tell", the strongest pairing and freshest delivery standing for an agent, a fingerprint-shaped name refused, channel rows carrying no evidence text. Integration tests go through the real device check: an owner phone is served with no planted id, path, fingerprint, topic id, evidence text or token; the token is served; an MSP phone, a demoted phone, a removed person's phone, a revoked, unsigned or tampered request, the notification token and a POST are refused, and the 21st read in a minute gets 429. A production-path test boots the server, pairs a phone, reads all three routes, finds a plan created through the real tracker, matches the channel rows to `/channels`, and confirms the raw routes stay closed to the phone. A migration test confirms existing agents get the awareness line once.

`tests/integration/recommended-services-confirm.test.ts`, `tests/integration/recommended-services-confirm-wiring.test.ts`, `tests/unit/recommended-services-ledger.test.ts`, `tests/e2e/recommended-services-alive.test.ts`. An independent review found three blocking problems, which were fixed and re-read.
