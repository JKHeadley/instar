## What Changed

A scheduled job with a gate that was running in its session form (a script job with its script runner off or on hold) was refused on every fire. After the gate, the scheduler compared the live job with the temporary session copy of it, which can never match, and reported `post-gate-definition-changed`. It now compares with the definition from before that copy was made. A job really edited during its gate is still refused. Refusals now name their cause: a new `definition-changed` skip reason, or `disabled`, `job-missing` or `portable-integrity`. A portable integrity failure is only recorded when the portable check failed. `definition-changed` also counts toward the alert for jobs that have gone quiet.

Routine automated messages now enter the dashboard Notifications feed unless their category is explicitly opted into push. Reply and conversation-serving floors remain. Quiet success has a durable receipt and does not clear a waiting reply or generate a mobile push. The gate covers direct sends, signed relays, recovery and lifeline delegation. Overseers keep housekeeping in handoffs and own bounded checks before requesting operator action.

`scripts/safe-merge.mjs` and the green-PR merge watcher no longer refuse or hold a pull request whose code owner has already approved it, when GitHub leaves the overall review decision empty. GitHub does that when the code-owner requirement comes from a ruleset. An empty decision now means "read the reviews": every owned file needs a named owner whose latest review approves the exact head. A stated decision still stands, and GitHub still enforces the rule at merge.

## What to Tell Your User

Some of my scheduled background jobs had stopped running since the last update, even though they looked fine, including the ones that work through feedback and turn it into fixes. They are running again now, so anything that was waiting on them will start moving.

Automated reports stay in Notifications. Choose any categories you want pushed from the dashboard; changing them requires your PIN. Normal conversations continue as usual, including text and media sent from your phone. Those copies do not clear a waiting reply. Other agents retain the staged operator-flip rollout; Echo follows its existing enforcement directive.

A rule-book change you have already approved on GitHub now merges by itself. Before, I could keep saying it was waiting for your review, or refuse to merge it, even though your approval was in place.
