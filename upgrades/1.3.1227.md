## What Changed

Output-disabled PromiseBeacon commitments now keep their internal heartbeat and
session-recovery cadence alive during quiet hours and after the human-output LLM
budget is exhausted. Those policies still suppress every user-facing send,
summary generation, and Attention action; only internal owner-side bookkeeping
and the existing bounded revival ladder continue. Standby machines remain inert.

Window 32 work receipts also use one validated lifecycle-authority timestamp for
both authorization and receipt stamping. This fixes false expiry when a
deterministic or injected lifecycle clock differs from the host wall clock, and
it makes the exact run ceiling consistently closed.

## What to Tell Your User

Overnight autonomous work can now keep its internal proof of life current even
while notifications are quiet or the messaging budget is exhausted. It stays
silent, avoids extra language-model spend, and only the owning machine may act.

## Summary of New Capabilities

- Silent internal commitment heartbeats continue through output-only suppressors.
- Existing owner-only session recovery remains available without opening any
  human-facing delivery path.
- Autonomous work receipts share the lifecycle clock and refuse the exact expiry
  boundary consistently.

## Evidence

The booted production-lifecycle harness reproduced the clock split as a work
advance rejected with HTTP 409 while the injected lifecycle clock still placed
the run inside its window. With the correction, that same receipt is accepted
and stamped from the single authority sample; an otherwise changed artifact at
the exact ceiling is rejected with HTTP 409. Booted-server PromiseBeacon tests
also held quiet hours and the spend cap active while observing owner heartbeat
or revival state advance, with zero sends, zero summary calls, zero Attention
actions, and no standby mutation. The final repository-wide test gate exited
zero; its E2E tier passed 351 files and 3,154 tests.
