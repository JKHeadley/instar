## What Changed

The Instar iPhone app can now show the agent's accounts, spending and resource use natively. Three new read-only routes answer a paired owner phone (or the agent's token): `GET /mobile/accounts` (each subscription account once, with its login email as the console shows it, signed in or needing sign-in on each machine, usage windows highest first with reset times, and how old the reading is; accounts needing sign-in first), `GET /mobile/spend?window=today|week|month` (recorded pay-per-use cost with how each figure was worked out, added across the machines that answer with each machine's own state, this machine's paid routes with committed spend against their limits and whether limits enforce, conversation token totals, and exactly what period each figure covers) and `GET /mobile/resources` (CPU and memory for the agent, its server and each session on this machine over the last hour, with old readings marked by age). They are trimmed summaries of what the console's Accounts, What thinking costs and Resource usage views show: no login folders, machine or account ids, credential references or raw errors. Only owner phones may read them (an MSP phone gets 403 `owner-phone-required`); they share their own 20-per-minute allowance, and pool-wide answers are built at most once every 15 seconds. The raw `/subscription-pool`, `/routing-spend/*`, `/tokens/*` and `/resources/*` routes stay closed to phones. A failed read records a `MobileCapacityScreens.read` degradation. The console's pool account view now names a peer's accounts by the machine that was asked, never by a name the peer supplied.

## What to Tell Your User

Once the app update with these screens is on your phone, your agent's page in the app has a "Capacity" section: Accounts, What thinking costs, and Resource usage. Accounts that need signing in are at the top, each account shows how much of its 5-hour and weekly limits is used and when they reset, the cost screen adds up what AI use cost across your machines, and the resource screen shows what is using the computer. If a machine is offline or didn't answer, the screen says so. These screens only look: signing in and spending limits stay in the dashboard. Only the owner's phone sees them.

## Summary of New Capabilities

- The owner's phone can see every subscription account, whether it is signed in on each machine, and its usage and reset times.
- The owner's phone can see what AI use cost across machines, how each figure was worked out, and whether spending limits are enforcing.
- The owner's phone can see CPU and memory use for the agent, its server and each session.

## Evidence

Unit tests cover both sides of every rule in the summaries: each sign-in state (including a failed login check and a repair in progress), usage windows and their tone and staleness lines, peer readings dated in the future, in-use only for this machine's rows, offline and unreached machines scoping the headline, spend basis words and unpriced rows, merging across machines with per-machine states and the state precedence, the paid-route designated machine and committed figures booked elsewhere, every limits sentence, resource staleness and ended sessions, the 15-second shared read, and the owner-or-token check. Integration tests go through the real device check: an owner phone is served with no folder, id, credential reference or raw error text; the token is served; an MSP phone, a demoted phone, a removed person's phone, a revoked, unsigned or tampered request (including a changed query), the notification token and a POST are refused, and the 21st read in a minute gets 429. A production-path test boots the server, pairs a phone, reads all three routes, checks the spend state against the server's own spend view, and confirms the raw routes stay closed to the phone. A migration test confirms existing agents get the awareness line once.
