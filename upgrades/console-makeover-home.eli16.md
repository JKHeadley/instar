# Console makeover, explained simply

Instar's dashboard has 29 tabs, sorted into groups by which part of Instar they belong to. Each one works, but nothing tells you at
a glance whether everything is fine or whether something needs you. You have to
know which tab to open.

We studied three newer agent apps (ChatGPT Dots, Meta Muse, Grok Bot). All three
always show what the agent is doing and what it is allowed to do. Instar records
more of that than any of them, and shows almost none of it.

The plan: a new console beside the old dashboard. Its first screen, Home, answers
six questions: can I reach you, what needs you, what I'm doing, what I've
promised, where I'm running, and what I'm allowed to do. Anything it cannot read
says "Can't tell" instead of pretending all is well. The 29 tabs are sorted into five
groups named after the question they answer; tapping one opens the old tab,
which works exactly as before. It uses the same colours, fonts and look as the
instar.sh website.

The old dashboard stays the default until Justin says the new one is good enough.
After that the new console takes over. The old one stays available until
everything it does has a working replacement, with no fixed deadline.
