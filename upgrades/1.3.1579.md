## What Changed

The Telegram origin store no longer grows without limit, and its minute-by-minute housekeeping no longer blocks replies. Archiving reads forward from a saved position instead of re-reading every row, writes batches of 100 records instead of a file per minute, and merges existing tiny archive files. After seven days a message's full detail lives only in the archive, while a small searchable record stays in the store. Metric events fold into totals. Audit records older than 365 days are removed by default, and audit reads say how far back history reaches (`historyExpiredBefore`). Change the limit with `messageOrigin.auditRetentionDays` in the Telegram messaging config. The file does not shrink by itself; freed space is reused.

## What to Tell Your User

Replies should no longer get held because my message history got too big. Old history moves into compact archives and can still be looked up. History older than a year is removed, and you can change that limit if you want to keep it longer. The store file stays at its current size rather than shrinking, but it stops growing.

## Summary of New Capabilities

No new capability. Retention limits for the origin store, one new setting (`messageOrigin.auditRetentionDays`, at least 30, default 365), and a new `historyExpiredBefore` field on audit listings.

## Evidence

Measured on read-only copies of the Studio store (644 MB, 55,943 origins, 569,331 metric events, 18,662 archive files). Before: the archive candidate query alone took 3 to 9.6 s on the single store worker, once a minute. After: once settled, each retention step takes under 100 ms. 312 sampled audit records read back byte-identical and federated totals match, apart from operations that legitimately expired during the replay. Live data fell to 123 MB.
