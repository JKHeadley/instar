## What Changed

A new constitutional article — **Never Silently Cut the Data a Decision Depends On** — plus the guard and the four fixes that earned it.

The crystallizing case: the cross-model reviewer's context budget was 60 KB, smaller than an ordinary spec. So the spec alone consumed the whole allowance and every referenced document — the parent design, the standards registry, the lessons doc — was dropped in FULL, on every round, of every review the machinery had ever run. Six rounds on `placement-real-capacity-scoring` were conducted with no reviewer ever seeing the design it was built on.

**The disclosure layer was not the failure, and that is the finding.** It ordered docs by value, kept the most important first, named every dropped document in the prompt, and told the reviewer its view was partial. All of that worked. The reviews happened anyway, because nothing REFUSED — and because "truncated to fit" is what a 100% loss says when the vocabulary only has a word for partial loss.

The article states three obligations that the existence of a bound does not discharge: the number is DERIVED (from what the consumer accepts and the producer emits, with the derivation recorded beside it — and *which end* to keep is derived too, since `slice(0, N)` discards the newest content of anything chronological); the cut is DISCLOSED IN-BAND to the consumer, not to a log nobody reads in time; and the operation REFUSES when the cut removes the input its correctness depends on.

Shipped with it: `src/core/boundedInput.ts` (keeps the correct end, writes the disclosure into the value, refuses a bound too small to hold that disclosure); the reviewer's `context-incomplete` refusal, which degrades before spending the model call; a transport-derived 256 KB budget; four converted sites; and a shrink-only lint in the blocking chain.

## What to Tell Your User

Nothing changes in how you talk to your agent, and nothing here can act on you.

If you send your own designs out for an outside opinion, one thing is visibly different: when the reviewer cannot be given the documents it is supposed to check your design against, it now says so and declines instead of returning a verdict. A review that comes back saying its context was incomplete is not a failure — it is the system telling you that the answer it would otherwise have given was not worth what it looked like. Usually the fix is a smaller design document. Occasionally a supporting document is simply larger than the whole budget on its own, and then no amount of trimming your design will help; the reviewer says that too, in those words, so you are not left following advice that cannot work.

## Summary of New Capabilities

- Cross-model reviews refuse rather than answer when the context they must check against did not arrive whole — naming exactly which documents were missing.
- The reviewer's context budget is 4x larger and derived from a stated constraint rather than an unexamined constant.
- A latent bug fixed where a size limit discarded the newest content and kept the oldest, in the check that detects a session stuck at a yes/no prompt — the question sits at the bottom, which is exactly what was being thrown away. The same defect was found and fixed in thread summarization, though an independent review established that code path has no caller today, so it was never live.

## Evidence

- **Converging audit, 2 rounds, validator-stamped:** `docs/audits/truncated-judgment-input.md`. Four independent search angles. The first angle found 18 sites and would have been registered as the population — the second angle proved its assumption false and surfaced two live bugs only it could see.
- **Both were the same defect in its purest form.** The thread summarizer selected the newest 20 messages then cut from the top, keeping the oldest of the twenty. The blocked-session detector took the terminal's last 20 lines then cut from the top — discarding the bottom, which is exactly where a blocking prompt appears; it could throw away the question it exists to find and confidently report nothing was wrong.
- **A self-review found a third.** A bound landing inside an astral character left a lone surrogate, which has no valid UTF-8 encoding — a bounded input arriving malformed. Fixed and pinned.
- **A test found a fourth, in the new helper.** At exactly the disclosure floor the retained slice was `slice(-0)` — the whole string — so the one function whose job is to hold a bound exceeded it at its own boundary.
- Tests: 17 helper + 9 ratchet (including a proof it bites on a re-introduced regression) + 5 assembly/refusal unit + 3 integration + strengthened coverage on both converted gates.
- Enforcement registered as **PARTIAL** in the article itself, on the operator's ruling: 7 fixed, 1 accepted with a written reason, **45** baselined shrink-only, ~95 classified out of scope. What is guaranteed today is no NEW instance, not no instance. *(The baseline was 26 until an independent review found the detector desynchronised by a stray backtick in a comment and evadable by hoisting the bound into a named constant — the style this standard prescribes. The population grew because the check got honest.)*
