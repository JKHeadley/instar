# Keep Approval Cards Clear

An approval card should let you decide in a few seconds. It should tell you what will change and what your approval means. If an agent fills the card with internal codes instead, the board points the writer to the field that needs clearer words.

## What you will see

A card for the screen check can say that every proposed screen change will show fresh pictures and a result you can read. Its description says why that matters. Its completion item says what you will be able to see when the work is finished. Its approval explanation tells you what your tap allows.

## What the board does with an unclear draft

The board points out the exact field that contains a recognized internal term, code or reference. The writer can explain the outcome in ordinary words and try again. The board still keeps the separate link or original source so someone working on the project can trace where the request came from.

## What your approval means

Your original request remains in your own words wherever the board has verified its source. Your approval keeps its existing meaning. The check helps catch known writing problems before you see a draft, and people still need to review whether the card gives you enough information to make a decision.
