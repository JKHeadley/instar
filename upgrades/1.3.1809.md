## What Changed

Local Verify keeps a clock, `mainObs.oldestUnverifiedAt`: the oldest merge not yet covered by a green `main` verify. Past three hours `main` becomes `main-suspect` (`unverified-too-long`) and only repairs may merge. A green verify moved that clock only when the verified head was still current at the end, or when the run had started on the then-current head (#3170). With a merge or a release commit every 20 to 30 minutes and a 35-minute verify that waits its turn for the slot, neither is usually true. On 2026-11-15 three green `main` verifies ran between 09:51Z and 14:26Z, the clock stayed at the first merge after boot, and `main` was `main-suspect` from 13:38Z; it flipped back 14 seconds after the 14:26Z green. Passed PRs sat at `main-suspect · repair-only`.

The service now remembers, per head, when a different head was first observed after it (`noteHeadSuperseded`, first observation kept, 50 heads). A green verify of head A covers every merge up to A, so the clock moves to the time A was replaced (`supersededAtFor`, which also follows A's release commit when that took its place), through a new fourth argument of `oldestUnverifiedAfterGreen`. It never moves backwards, and a head never seen replaced changes nothing.

One existing behaviour is refined: a green re-run of an older head used to leave the clock untouched; it now moves to the moment that head was replaced (earlier than the re-run's start, so still strict).

No config, state or migration. The replacement times live in memory and are rebuilt after a restart, when the boot verify is of the current head anyway.

- **A held reply is no longer ended because a newer message exists.** A reply that could not be sent at once (Telegram unreachable, or the machine that sends to Telegram was away) used to be thrown away when it was finally retried if anything newer had appeared in the topic, including the same session's own "got it, looking into this now". The user got nothing and nobody was told (F1 in `docs/specs/native-conversation-base.md`). The timestamp review is removed on every agent with no setting. A held reply is now delivered when it can be. The only reply that is still ended is one whose exact text Telegram already accepted in the same place.
- **A late reply says so in its first line.** A session reply sent more than 5 minutes after it was written starts with "Late reply, written more than N minutes ago and held up in delivery. It answers an earlier message:". The session that wrote it gets one line saying it went out late, and whether the first line could be added.
- **Every ending has a reason, and the author is told.** A suppressed reply now always carries one of five stated reasons. A reply that definitely did not arrive is handed back to the session that wrote it, with one of three causes ("Telegram refused it", "it could not be sent before its attempts ran out", "it passed its delivery deadline") and a route to read its text (`GET /telegram/origins/operations/ID/text`, API token only). A reply whose delivery could not be confirmed gets the uncertain line ("It may have arrived. Do not send it again unless the user says it is missing.") and is never sent twice. The two topic notices are reworded so the uncertain one never says "did not reach you".
- **Older machines.** When the machine that sends to Telegram runs the previous release and ends a reply with no reason, the reply is replaced once with fresh times; a second reasonless ending leaves it unresolved, tells the session to check the conversation before repeating it, and keeps one record-only attention item per topic until it is resolved. After a lease change such a reply is never sent without the previous owner's own record.
- **Status.** `GET /telegram/origins/status` gains `heldReplies` (`lateDeliveries`, `recoveredUnderThreshold`, `lateUnmarked`, `ownerUnstated`, `definiteNoSession`, `neverHandedBack`), read at the 2026-11-25 review.

- **"Did my held reply arrive?" now gives the sending machine's own answer, from any machine.** On an agent with more than one machine, the held-reply outcome check used to answer only from the asking machine's own records. When another machine had done the send and ended the reply, this machine said "unknown, not safe to resend" while the sending machine knew the answer (F2 in `docs/specs/native-conversation-base.md`). The check now asks the machine that sends to Telegram (the execution owner) over the existing signed machine link, using the `receipt` verb with a new `reader: true` flag, and asks the machine that wrote the reply whether it still holds it. It then applies one fixed table: a delivery anywhere is delivered; a duplicate ending is delivered; a newer copy is followed to its own answer; the owner's failure with the writer no longer holding it is not-delivered (with the cause); anything missing is `unknown` with the missing fact named in `why`. It never says "safe to resend" on missing evidence.
- **The answer says who gave it.** New fields: `answeredBy`, `asOf`, `ownerAnswered`, `why` (`owner-unresolved`, `owner-no-record`, `author-unanswered`, `timeout`, `refused`, `old-version`, `owner-read-off`), `reason`, `failure` (`refused`, `never-left`, `deadline`), `supersededBy`, `duplicateOfMessageId`. Existing fields are unchanged.
- **Suppression reasons are read, not guessed.** A suppressed reply with no stated reason (every one written before this release) is now `unknown`; before, it read "not delivered, safe to resend", which invited a duplicate when the reply had been ended as a duplicate. Quiet notices and retired historical notices read "not delivered, ended on purpose" with `safeToResend: false`.
- **Bounded.** The route answers within 4 s (the reply script waits 5 s). Each ask gets at most 2 s, owner and writer are asked in parallel, concurrent asks for one reply share one call, a machine that just timed out is answered at once for 2 s, and a final owner verdict is cached (1024 entries). A held reply that was never handed to another machine makes no network call.
- **Switch.** `messageOrigin.ownerRead.enabled` (default on, read live, never written) stops this machine asking; its answers then fall to the cautious row (`unknown`, `why: owner-read-off`). `messageOrigin.forwardToHolder.enabled: false` has the same effect. Tuning: `ownerRead.budgetMs`, `ownerRead.cacheEntries`.
- **The reply script follows `safeToResend`.** A `not-delivered` answer with `safeToResend: false` now prints "ended on purpose ... Do NOT resend it" instead of "safe to send it again". The previous script version's hash is registered so installed copies are replaced on update.
- **Status.** `GET /telegram/origins/status` gains `ownerRead`: ask, timeout, refusal, old-version and cache counts, ask and route p95 latency, and `routeOverBudget`. A machine without the reader shows `state: unknown`.

- **A tone suggestion on a held reply now reaches the session that wrote it.** When a reply was forwarded to the machine that sends to Telegram, or queued for a retry, and the outbound check answered with a suggestion, nobody was told: the reply sat held, was reviewed again on every retry, and could be retired without ever being seen (F1 in `docs/specs/native-conversation-base.md`; cause in `docs/specs/reports/ncb-f1-cause.md`). Now the machine holding it ends its own copy in the same step that records the verdict, and the session that wrote the reply gets one line: "Your reply `ID` was not sent. The outbound check has a suggestion (`RULE`): ..." with the deadline and how to answer. Nothing sends it except the session's answer.
- **Answering it.** A reply sent with `--tone-complied RULE --tone-decision-ref REF` (a revised version) or `--tone-ack RULE --tone-reason "why" --tone-decision-ref REF` (as written) replaces the held one: the original is withdrawn in the same transaction before anything is sent, so the user gets exactly one message. A reply without the reference is a separate message. An answer to a reply that already ended sends nothing and the script prints that reply's recorded outcome.
- **A refusal in custody ends the reply.** A credential, a loopback link, a blocking rule or a deterministic floor catch on a held reply now ends it with one line ("the outbound check refused it (`RULE`) ... Rewrite it before sending again.") instead of being retried. A review that produced no verdict (capacity shed, outage, timeout) is retried exactly as before.
- **No more double sends after "not sent".** The machine that sends to Telegram used to answer "not sent" while keeping its own copy queued, so the original could be delivered after the session sent a revision. It now answers "not sent" only after ending its copy; an older machine's answer is treated as "still held".
- **Status.** `GET /telegram/origins/status` → `heldReplies` gains `advisoryHandedBack`, `advisoryLineSent`, `advisoryNoSession`, `advisoryComplied`, `advisoryAcked`, `advisoryAnswerRefused`, `advisoryExpiredUnanswered`, `advisoryUnmatchedReply`, `policyRefusedHandedBack`, `advisoryAnswerDelayCount` and `advisoryAnswerDelaySumSeconds`, read at the 2026-11-25 review.

## What to Tell Your User

Nothing changes for you. A development safeguard had been closing merging every three hours on busy days even though the main line was being tested and passing; it now counts those tests correctly.

If one of my replies gets held up in delivery, it now still reaches you when the connection comes back, instead of being dropped because the conversation moved on. If it arrives more than five minutes late it starts with a short line saying it is late and which earlier message it answers. If a reply could not be delivered at all, I am told and can send it again.

When one of my replies was held up and I check whether it reached you, any of my machines now gets the answer from the machine that actually sent it, instead of saying "I can't tell". I still never send something twice when the answer is unclear.

If the check I run on my own messages suggests a change to a reply that was waiting to be delivered, I now actually see that suggestion and either fix the reply or send it as written. Before, such a reply could sit unseen and never reach you, and in rare cases you could have received two versions of it. Now you get exactly one.

## Summary of New Capabilities

| Capability | How to Use |
|-----------|------------|
| The unverified-main clock follows green verifies of heads that were replaced before the run ended | Automatic |

- Held replies are delivered late and marked instead of being ended on a timestamp.
- Session lines for late, definitely failed and unconfirmed replies; `GET /telegram/origins/operations/ID/text` for the reply text.
- `heldReplies` counters on the origin status.

- The held-reply outcome route asks the execution owner and the writing machine, and answers with `answeredBy`, `why` and the delivery cause.
- `receipt` mesh verb: optional `reader: true` for any same-agent machine (state, reason and message ids only, never message text).
- `messageOrigin.ownerRead.enabled` switch; `ownerRead` counters on the origin status.

- A held reply with a tone suggestion is handed back to its session with one line, and waits for its answer until its deadline.
- Answering with the decision reference replaces the held reply exactly once.
- Advisory counters on the origin status and the federated metrics.
