## What Changed

The design-review process gains two principles at the operator's request (2026-11-18, topic 145097):
- **P25, A Safeguard Must Leave a Benefit.** Every restriction names its cost, and something useful must survive all of them.
- **P26, Documents Stand on Their Own.** Design documents are written for a reader with no context.

Both are also constitution articles in `docs/STANDARDS-REGISTRY.md` (A Safeguard Must Leave a Benefit; Documents Stand on Their Own), ratified by the operator on 2026-11-18.

The spec-converge lessons-aware reviewer asks both on every spec (Q6), and the ELI16 overview template opens with the stands-on-its-own rule. No runtime change.

A conversation the agent creates for its owner no longer needs the owner to type in it before an everyday change to it can be made (bug fb-ac3d2e79-2a4).

The topic-profile propose lane accepts `confirmIn`: the conversation where the owner asked. The server posts the exact change there ("For the conversation "<name>": …"), and the owner's "yes" there applies it to the other conversation.
- **Fields:** model, model tier, thinking mode and effort only.
- **Eligibility:** the owner must be the configured owner, the asking conversation must be their own, and the target must be unbound or theirs and not a team member's. This is checked when the proposal is made and again on the yes.
- **Switch:** ships live on development agents, dark on the fleet (`security.crossTopicConfirm.enabled`).

`POST /telegram/topics` also accepts `onBehalfOf`, a message the agent links as the reason it created a conversation. It is recorded privately, marked unverified, and grants nothing.

The worktree monitor now keeps the first 50 changed-file names for each branch with unmerged work, plus a count of all of them, instead of the full list. Branches far behind main used to list every file main had changed since then. On one development machine the saved report grew to 314 MB and was rewritten every five minutes, stalling the server for about 0.7 seconds each time (fb-411b9b0c-6a6). The notice still shows five names and now takes its "+N more" from the count.

The worktree checker in the agent server (every 5 minutes, and after each session ends) now runs one scan at a time. If a scan is still running when the next one is due, the new request waits for it and shares its result instead of starting a second scan. On agent homes with hundreds of worktrees, scans could take longer than 5 minutes on a busy machine and overlap three at a time, each starting a burst of git processes; that load is gone. Reports and alerts are unchanged.

## What to Tell Your User

Nothing changes in how your agent behaves day to day. Designs for new Instar features are now checked for two more things: that their safety limits still leave a real benefit, and that their documents can be read by someone with no background.

When you ask me, in one conversation, to change how another one runs (like its model), I'll show you the exact change right where you asked, and your "yes" there makes it, even in a conversation I just started for you. It only works for conversations that are yours.

Nothing to do. A background check that could slow the agent's server on machines with many old work copies is now lightweight.

- **Less background load**: "On machines with a lot of work in progress, the agent's routine check of its working copies no longer runs several times over at once, so the machine stays more responsive."

## Evidence

The principles text is in `docs/INSTAR-DESIGN-PRINCIPLES-AND-LESSONS.md` (P25, P26), and the reviewer question is Q6 in `skills/spec-converge/templates/reviewer-lessons-aware.md`. Related tests pass, 99/99.

- **Unit:** the eligibility checks both ways; the write surface path; the slot target; the switch.
- **Integration:**
  - the proposal is armed in the owner's conversation, and nothing is written before the yes;
  - each refusal, with the owner told when the target is the problem;
  - the dark switch.
- **E2E:** the routes through the production wiring.

New unit tests in `tests/unit/worktree-monitor.test.ts` cover both sides of the cap. A branch with 65 changed files keeps 50 names and counts 65. A branch with 2 changed files keeps both. The file passes 35 of 35. Measurement behind the fix: the Studio's 314 MB report took 261 ms to stringify and 425 ms to write, in a separate process.

Tests: `tests/unit/worktree-monitor.test.ts` adds overlapping callers sharing one report, a fresh scan after the shared one settles, and a failed scan not blocking the next; 32 passed. Measured on the Mac Studio (345 worktrees, load 40+): about three overlapping scans per server before the change.
